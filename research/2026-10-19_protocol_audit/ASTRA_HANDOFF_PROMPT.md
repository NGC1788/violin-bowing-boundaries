## 6. Results of round-3 literature check (bifurcation-aware extrapolation; raw JSON `~/violin-rne/pilot_few_stroke/novelty_check3_raw.json`)

Verdict of both branches: **partial overlap** — the pieces exist, the combination does not.

- **Finite-window bias of measured regime boundaries is NOT new** in general: glass-transition Tg (relaxation time > experiment time), pipe-flow critical Re (Mukund & Hof 2018, >10^7 advective times), yield-stress fluidisation times. In bowed strings, **Schoonderwaldt et al. 2008 already noted it qualitatively**: long transients and Helmholtz/multiple-slip alternation near F_min, "classification depends on where in the steady part the sample is taken", slow fluctuations persisting >1 s, and T_steady 0.65–4.0 s that was *shorter at higher speeds* (31 cm stroke limit) — **the same speed–settling confound as the IWK data**. They concluded "T_steady sufficient" and did not correct. This makes their "F_min independent of speed" result itself suspect — a strong, citable point.
- **Extrapolating a threshold from critical slowing down with a bifurcation-type exponent exists**: Meisel, Klaus, Kuehn & Plenz 2015 (PLoS Comput Biol 11:e1004097) fit recovery rate λ = a(V_c − V)^θ and found θ = 0.5 (saddle-node) predicts the spiking threshold. Related: Radisson & Kanso 2023 (PRE), D'Souza, Epureanu & Pascual 2015 (PLoS ONE, large-perturbation recoveries), flutter damping extrapolation. They use small-perturbation recovery on the stable side, not censored binary regime labels.
- **Settling-convention dependence of F_min exponents exists in simulation**: Beigi & Conneely arXiv 2609.14990 v3 §II-H — β exponent of the A string moves between −1.14 and −1.58 depending on fixed-seconds vs fixed-periods windows; Helmholtz cell count changes 38 % with 140 settling periods; with fixed-seconds windows the G-string boundary vanishes at 7/10 positions. They still conclude β⁻¹ "when properly settled (140 periods)". Our extrapolated −2.16 conflicts with this; our argument: near the boundary the relaxation time diverges (A2: τ ≈ 2.5 s at 0.05–0.10 m/s, longer than 140 periods ≈ 1.4 s), so a fixed settling budget is never enough there.
- **Bifurcation type of the F_min boundary is not established** in the literature (Gourc et al. 2022 JSV 516 links F_min to "classical and non-smooth" bifurcations without naming the type; Schoonderwaldt §4.4 describes a secondary slip growing after the main slip as force decreases). Plausibly a non-smooth (grazing/sticking-sliding) bifurcation, where critical slowing down is NOT guaranteed.
- **Gap that survives (state the contribution narrowly):** (a) lock-in time from time-resolved labels treated as interval-/right-censored survival data; (b) its time scale parametrised as τ ∝ (F − F*)^(−θ) with F* and θ estimated jointly; (c) **discriminating the bifurcation type from data** — compare θ = 1/2 (saddle-node / type-I intermittency), θ = 1 (Hopf / period-doubling / Neimark–Sacker) and "non-divergent" (non-smooth grazing) within one likelihood (AIC / likelihood ratio) — this is what would distinguish the work from Meisel 2015; (d) an explicit non-identifiability rule when τ(F) exceeds the observation time; (e) the first quantitative correction of robot-bowing Schelleng boundaries.

### 10.2 The likely reason: they measured a different boundary
Original text (`~/violin-bowing-boundaries/work/schoonderwaldt2008_pdf/schoonderwaldt2008.pdf`, §2.4 and Fig. 1; text in `~/violin-rne/claude_extra/s2008.txt`): bow strokes were designed so that "Helmholtz motion should be developed in the initial phase", the attack acceleration–force combination "was chosen to obtain short pre-Helmholtz transients", and "during the transition phase T_trans the force was smoothly changed to the target force". → Schoonderwaldt's F_min is a **maintenance boundary** (Helmholtz already present, force lowered to target). ### 10.12 ⚠️ MAJOR (Claude, 2026-10-18 01:05): at low forces the IWK strokes are NOT constant-force — the bow force decays during the stroke
Source: `claude_extra/ramp_meta_A2.csv` (all 6,000 A2 strokes; force_at_onset = median force in the 10 ms before the bow starts) + per-window force in `timeres/windows_full.csv.gz`; script `claude_extra/force_history.py`.
- **Acceleration design** (`ramp_analysis.py`): linear ramps (R² = 1.000); acceleration 5–95 % range 0.026–6.0 m/s² (v1), 0.10–6.1 (v2), 0.42–6.2 (v3); rank correlation with β +0.69…+0.87; **within 23 of 40 β columns acceleration rises perfectly with force rank (ρ = +1.00)**, the other columns are constant (low-force columns use a fixed 4.8 cm ramp distance, a ∝ v²). So acceleration is a deterministic design function of (v, β, F): **a ≈ max(v²/(2·0.048 m), k(v)·F_onset^1.0–1.1·β^2.0–2.6)** — 45/52/68 % of v1/v2/v3 strokes sit on the 4.8 cm-ramp floor (0.026/0.104/0.417 m/s²); the rest follow the power law with R² 0.95–0.97 (max 17–20 m/s²). Near the lower boundary (small β, low force) the attack is the floor, i.e. **16× gentler at 0.05 m/s than at 0.20 m/s**. Figure: `claude_extra/fig_force_decay.png` (top: force vs time by force rank; bottom: acceleration map β × force rank).
- **Force decay** (median over force-rank bins; onset → first plateau window → last plateau window, N):

  In ranks 1–20 every stroke ends below half its starting force; the lowest ranks lose contact (≈ 0 N). The lower boundary (≈ 0.1–0.5 N) lies exactly in this region. van Walstijn 2026 mentions only "a small normal load reduction" and uses the window mean; POMA 2025 does not mention it. (Mechanism unknown; the robot runs position-controlled — "not configured to adjust its movement to the sensed normal force" — so geometry along the bow travel is a candidate.)
- **Consequences:** (i) near the lower boundary IWK strokes are partly *force-descending* strokes, i.e. part establishment, part maintenance — the very distinction of §10.2; (ii) the time trends we attributed to settling (β exponent steepening with time, §4; critical slowing down, lock-in later near the boundary) are confounded with a force that keeps falling during the plateau; (iii) per-window force (what `replicate.py` stores) must be used, never the nominal force, and the *history* (force at onset, acceleration) must enter the model.
- **Natural experiment for hysteresis in measured data** (`force_history.py`): last plateau window per stroke (late half), logistic P(H) with flexible terms in current force F(t) and β plus h·log(F_onset/F(t)); stroke bootstrap (200):
  - v2: h = +13.9 [+4.6, +23.5]; with log acceleration added +13.7 [+4.9, +22.4]
  - v3: h = +11.4 [+2.8, +20.8]; with acceleration +5.2 [−3.3, +15.4]
  - v1: h = −3.6 [−7.8, +0.7]; with acceleration −1.6 [−5.4, +1.9]
  → At the same current force, strokes that *started* at a higher force are more often Helmholtz at 0.10 and 0.20 m/s — the sign expected from establishment/maintenance hysteresis — but not at 0.05 m/s. **Not evidence** (see the drift caveat below): the history variable is set by the design (collinear with β/force rank), reverse causation is possible (the regime could change the measured normal force), and the logistic is crude. Needs: matched pairs (same β column, same current force, different onset force), a time-resolved version (does Helmholtz persist after F(t) falls below the column's establishment threshold?), and replication on A1 (`claude_extra/ramp/ramp_meta_2024-03-25_TypeA_sample1.csv`, streaming now) and later archives.
- **Real force change or load-cell drift? Not resolved — both matter.** Evidence collected so far: (a) the absolute decrease onset → plateau end is similar across force ranks (~0.14–0.23 N), i.e. additive and travel-dependent rather than proportional; (b) some low readings are negative (to −0.01 N) and the bridge-force DC of two lowest-force strokes drifts to the same −0.157 N and stays there after the bow has stopped — signs of baseline drift; (c) the reading at the end of a stroke with the bow stationary differs from the start (v1 trial 1: 0.22 → 0.013 N) whereas a 4 N stroke returns (4.27 → 3.94 → 4.32); (d) within a fixed force rank the Helmholtz fraction *rises* over the plateau (v1 ranks 11–20: 26 → 55 → 64 → 68 %) while the measured force falls (0.20 → 0.07 N); (e) windows with measured force < 0.03 N are still Helmholtz in 46 % of cases at v1, β ≈ 0.11–0.20 (2755/6025), 15/245 at β ≈ 0.02–0.035. A real decay (position-controlled path not parallel to the bow hair, with maintenance keeping Helmholtz alive) and a load-cell drift (true force ~constant, settling raises the Helmholtz fraction) both fit (a)–(e). **Consequently the positive history coefficient h above is NOT evidence for hysteresis**: under a drift, strokes whose reading fell more simply have a higher true force than their current reading. To separate the two, look for a physical force proxy independent of the load cells (e.g. pitch flattening or slip-phase duration vs force in Helmholtz windows at high force, then applied to low-force windows), or ask the user whether the authors can be asked.

### 10.13 RESOLVED (mostly): the low-force decrease is a real, travel-dependent force change — not a load-cell drift in time (Claude, 2026-10-18 01:50)
Data: `claude_extra/stroke_blocks.py` streamed every stroke of A2 and A1 into 50 ms blocks (`claude_extra/blocks/blocks_A2.npz`, `blocks_A1.npz`; fields speed, travelled position, force DC, force AC, bridge/nut DC, bridge p2p/edge); tests `claude_extra/drift_tests.py` (A1 needs `reverse_rank`: trial order is reversed there); figure `claude_extra/fig_drift_test.png`.
1. **Travel, not time.** Force change vs bow travel is identical for the three speeds (A2, force ranks 1–20: −0.044, −0.088, −0.125, −0.153, −0.174 N at 2, 5, 8, 11, 14 cm; between-speed spread 0.0016 N), whereas vs elapsed time the speeds separate (spread 0.040 N). A1: spread 0.0077 (travel) vs 0.0135 (time). A time drift (thermal, creep, electronics) would collapse on time.
2. **It stops when the bow stops.** Force rate while moving −0.02 … −0.18 N/s; after the bow has stopped +0.006 … +0.07 N/s (slight recovery); before the bow starts ≈ 0 (|rate| ≤ 0.003 N/s). Same in A1.
3. **Mechanical signatures.** At 10 cm travel the decrease is larger for higher force ranks (A2: −0.15 to −0.19 N at ranks 21–30, −0.23 to −0.31 N at ranks 41–50) and larger at small β in 18/18 rank × speed cells (A2 ratio small-β/large-β 1.21–1.36, consistent across cells). A position-controlled bow pressing with a hair ribbon of stiffness k_h in series with the string (k_s ∝ 1/(β(1−β))) predicts exactly this; the A2 ratio implies k_h ≈ 700 N/m and a path-to-hair mismatch of ≈ 0.4 mm per 10 cm (bow camber / path not parallel to the hair). A position-dependent sensor offset (e.g. cable drag) would not scale with force level or contact stiffness.
4. **Documented by the same lab:** Lampis, Mayer & Chatziioannou, Acta Acustica 8, 44 (2024), doi 10.1051/aacus/2024034 (`papers/Lampis2024_ActaAcustica_playability.pdf`, §2.1): "In practice, the bow force was not directly controlled. The elevation of the bow with respect to the string results in having different bow forces." They over-tensioned the hair and limited bowing to 6 cm in the middle of the hair to keep the force "almost constant"; the 2025 dataset strokes travel 15–20 cm. Also consistent: onset forces deviate from the nominal grid by factors 0.4–1.9 (open-loop elevation), differently in A1 and A2.
5. Session dependence: A1's decrease is ~3× smaller than A2's (bow remounted / hair tension / alignment differ) — another reason to use the measured per-window force, never the nominal grid.
6. **Separate artifact — bridge channel:** during constant-speed bowing the mean lateral force must be > 0 (v_b⟨F⟩ = string losses + contact dissipation), yet at the lowest forces the bridge DC is < −0.02 N in 61–90 % of plateau blocks (A2 ranks 1–10; typical minimum −0.07 to −0.10 N) and 26–31 % in A1 (≈ −0.03 N). This is a bridge-channel offset (cause unknown; not cross-talk from the vertical load, which reads ≈ 0 with the bow placed). It does not affect the shape-based regime labels but invalidates any DC/friction-ratio analysis at low force. The nut channel tracks the bridge only weakly (calibrated gain −0.14 … −0.32, r −0.17 … −0.57) and cannot arbitrate.
**Consequences (update to §10.12):** the IWK low-force strokes really are force-descending strokes (a mixed establishment → maintenance protocol), so the drift caveat on the natural-experiment hysteresis test is largely removed — but the design collinearity remains. Matched-*time* comparisons across speeds are also matched-*force-history* mismatched: at the same plateau time the 0.20 m/s strokes have travelled 4× further and lost more force. Use per-window measured force plus travel/onset-force covariates. Remaining uncertainty: a position-dependent load-cell error cannot be excluded with these data alone (items 3–4 make it unlikely).
