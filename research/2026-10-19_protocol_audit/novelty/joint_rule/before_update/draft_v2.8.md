## Abstract

Force-law comparisons depend on how measured waveforms are classified and converted into boundaries. We distinguish arbitrary label fragility from sensitivity attainable by a shared waveform decision rule in a public robotic cello-bowing collection. A specialized soft-regular dynamic program and interval optimization give exact minimum edits for reversing a specified first-K-run boundary-law compatibility decision. An event-complete threshold sweep and an independent record-envelope formulation then restrict changes to one scalar waveform threshold shared across all 78,000 windows. At K=2 and a declared log-force tolerance of 0.2, arbitrary edits restore a beta exponent of −2 in 14 of 39 folders, whereas the examined coherent rule families restore only one. In that folder, the minimum is 5 arbitrary edits, 6 deletion-only edits, or 44 eligible-window changes under the attainable ratio rule. Applying the same rule to the entire dataset changes 3,200 labels and makes originally finite boundaries unresolved in nine other folders. Exact enumeration covers all 182,057 threshold patterns; independent implementations reproduce every stored profile. A separate post hoc requirement to retain every originally resolved boundary eliminates all primary-tolerance restorations in the examined families, but this changes at a wider tolerance. These are deterministic sensitivity results, not true-label corrections or reversals of the earlier MLE confidence intervals. The contribution links unrestricted edit bounds, rule-attainable boundaries and collateral consequences in a reproducible scientific-analysis audit.

## 1. Introduction

Helmholtz motion of a bowed string occurs only between a minimum and a maximum bow force. Schelleng's analysis gives a maximum force proportional to Z v/β and a minimum force proportional to Z² v/(R β²) [1], with Z the characteristic impedance, v the bow speed, β the relative bow–bridge distance and R the termination resistance. Measurements and simulations disagree about the minimum force: it was nearly independent of bow speed on violin strings [2], had a non-zero baseline at very low speed [3], and scaled as Z v/β in a recent simulation preprint [4]. Theory describes the force needed to *sustain* Helmholtz motion [5], whereas a stroke starting from rest must first *establish* it; the attack (initial acceleration and force) controls establishment [6,7].

A public collection from the IWK (University of Music and Performing Arts Vienna) provides complete Schelleng diagrams for cello G strings of four constructions at two tensions, plus high-force sessions, recorded by a bowing robot [8,9]. It has been used to validate a thermal friction model [10]. Boundaries read from finite windows of individual strokes mix string physics with protocol: how force is applied, how the stroke starts, how long it has settled, and which strokes share a session.

This study reports what the collection can and cannot show. We contribute (i) a quantitative protocol audit, (ii) three sets of locally frozen, once-only tests of maximum-force laws (one primary success in the training force range, one failure in a new force range and new sessions), (iii) a post hoc minimum-force association that frozen tests did not confirm, and (iv) a negative prediction result. We also contribute a criterion-sensitivity analysis with explicit counterexamples and conditional feature replay (§§3.10–3.11), followed by an exact minimum-label-edit calculation for a specified operational law-compatibility decision (§3.12) and a coherent waveform-rule restriction with collateral accounting (§3.13). This application does not introduce a new interval-censoring estimator. Every result is labelled frozen or post hoc. "log" denotes the natural logarithm throughout.

## 2. Data and methods

**Data.** We used parts 1–8 of the IWK collection [9] (CC BY 4.0). The dataset paper calls each 2000-stroke single-speed scan a "Schelleng diagram" [8]; here a *diagram* is the 6000-stroke set of one string sample over three bow speeds (0.05, 0.10, 0.20 m/s) × 40 log-spaced bow positions (β = 0.02–0.20) × 50 log-spaced nominal forces (0.1–4 N). The sampling rate is 50 kHz (Zenodo record descriptions [9]). The **development set** is 11 diagrams: five at the lower tension T1 (A1, A2, B1, C1, D1) and six at T2 (A_T2, B_T2s1, C_T2, D_T2, and the same-day repeats B_T2r1/r2 from the variability set). D_T2 was held out for a frozen test and is now spent. A3 and A4 were included in later analyses: A3 was scored under FROZEN_v1, with its timing limitation stated in §3.2, and A4 was a frozen target (§3.3). Later minimum-force and criterion analyses use expanded sets, with their own denominators stated in §§3.8–3.13. Specifically, §§3.11–3.13 use 13 diagrams: the ten non-D_T2 development diagrams plus A3, A4 and D2 (78,000 windows). This reuses exposed data rather than adding independent observations. Z and bending stiffness EI are the published values [8]. The dataset paper documents that each string type was mounted new and that additional A_T1 samples and repeated measurements exist [8]; we count diagrams, not independent strings.

**High-force sessions (parts 7–8).** Each of four strings (C_T2, A_T2, D_T2, B_T2; "sample 2", recorded 2024-06-22/24/27/29) was scanned at the same three speeds × 30 log-spaced β (0.02–0.07) × 50 log-spaced forces (3.8–12.6 N measured). These are different sessions from the T2 development diagrams, and the force range starts where the development range ends, so they are not an extension of the same force sweep. The design was verified from control signals alone before any label was parsed (`highforce_confirm/controls_audit.json`).

**Labels and windows.** Each 0.2-s window is labelled by a fixed bridge-force waveform classifier (one flyback per period = "Helmholtz", H), with amplitude thresholds set once on A2. The label is a proxy for string motion. Primary windows start t = 0.2 s after the bow-speed plateau begins; the force of a window is its mean measured bow force.

**Boundaries.** In each speed × β column the strokes are sorted by window force. With K = 2 force-adjacent H strokes as a run, the lower boundary is the interval below the first run and the upper boundary the interval above the last run (the dataset paper uses K = 3 [8]). Boundaries outside the measured range are censored, not imputed. A column without any run ("no band") is recorded as right-censored for the lower boundary and unobservable for the upper boundary. This is an *assumption*: with binary labels and the same error rate outside the band, an H region entirely below and entirely above the measured range give identical likelihoods, so the binary data cannot identify the censoring direction (`astra_20261019/observation_model/README.md`). Laws log F = c + a log v + b log β + Δ_d are fitted by interval-censored Gaussian likelihood; Δ_d is a diagram's offset from a reference shape. Interval-censored likelihood is established methodology [21], not a new estimator introduced here. For §3.11, a stable log-CDF-difference implementation and convergence diagnostics are used; the previously frozen scores are not replaced.

**Load-decrease descriptor.** dec5_pl is label-free but measured on the same session as the boundary: for the 20 lowest nominal forces at each speed, the median change of the measured force between the first 0.1-s plateau window and the first window at least 5 cm of travel later (negative = decrease), averaged over speeds. At 0.05 m/s that later window starts about 1 s into the plateau, i.e. after the boundary window. It is a same-session covariate, not an a-priori predictor of an unseen string.

**Models (Sec. 3.4).** Trained on A1+A2: a model-free lookup (median boundary of the 5 nearest β columns at the same speed); Schelleng and Beigi forms with one constant; a free-exponent law with Z as a fixed offset; logistic regression and gradient boosting. Error = median |log(F_pred/F_mid)| over columns with finite intervals (F_mid = geometric interval midpoint), averaged over diagrams. This comparison followed a plan written beforehand (`final/ANALYSIS_PLAN_FINAL.md`) whose primary analysis used flyback-derived Z and few-shot adaptation at k = 10; the few-shot results were invalidated by a slope-fitting bug and the flyback Z was biased against the published values, so the published-Z comparison reported here is a post hoc change.

**Frozen tests and evidence level.** Plans and predictions were saved locally before the targets' labels were parsed and were scored once; none was publicly pre-registered.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Protocol audit (post hoc)

**Force control.** The dataset paper states that bow force was set "solely from the elevation of the bow" and remained "almost constant during the strokes" [8]; a later model study notes "a small normal load reduction" [10]. In A2, for nominal-force ranks 1–20, the measured force fell by 0.044, 0.088, 0.125, 0.153 and 0.174 N after 2, 5, 8, 11 and 14 cm of travel, nearly identically at the three speeds when plotted against travel (between-speed SD 0.0016 N; against time 0.040 N). In A1 the decrease was 2.5–4 times smaller and the travel collapse weaker (0.0077 vs 0.0135 N). While the bow moved the reading fell at 0.020–0.180 N/s; before it moved, by at most 0.0021 N/s.

**Attack schedule follows recording order.** Within every β column the attack acceleration rises with the *trial index* (within-column Spearman +0.98 to +1.00 in A1 and A2). A2 was scanned with β and force ascending, so there the acceleration also rises with force. A1 was scanned in reverse (β and force descending), so its acceleration *falls* with force (ρ −0.98 to −1.00). Consequently the 20 lowest-force strokes per column received a median attack of 0.563 m/s² in A1 at every speed, against 0.038, 0.104 and 0.417 m/s² in A2 (15×, 5.4× and 1.35× harsher in A1). Among the eight development diagrams with ramp metadata, A1 is the only reverse scan; the other seven share A2's schedule exactly (`paper/repro/attack_scan_all.log`). In A2, 45 %, 51 % and 67 % of strokes at the three speeds use a constant 4.8-cm ramp; for low-force strokes the 0.20 m/s attack is 16× harsher than at 0.05 m/s for β < 0.043, 11× at 0.043–0.093 and 1.4× above 0.093.

**Sessions and window placement.** Each speed is a separate complete β–force scan, so speed is confounded with recording block. The 0.4-s windows distributed with the dataset begin 1.55, 0.35 and 0.08 s after plateau onset at the three speeds (A2). A pooled censored fit to those windows (A1, B1, C1, D1, A2) gave a minimum-force speed exponent of +1.77 [1.64, 1.90]; aligning windows at equal time since plateau onset gave +0.57–0.67 (A1) and +0.94–1.04 (A2), and per-diagram matched-time exponents of +1.09 to +1.42 for the eight other diagrams. Because the force decrease collapses on travel and equal times mean unequal travel, this *window-placement* confound mixes settling time with travel-dependent force loss; it is not identified which.

**Low-force reliability.** Readings after contact was lost were not zero (folder medians −14.9 to +61.0 mN). In one A1 folder the post-contact reading varied from ≈ +4 mN (large β, recorded first) to ≈ +158 mN (small β, recorded last; correlation with trial index +0.95, with β −0.88). Recording order and β are confounded; across folders with opposite scan directions the offset falls with increasing β, which points to a pose-dependent offset rather than drift. In D_T2, strokes inside an H run read −23 to −27 mN. Forces below about 0.2 N are therefore uncertain by tens of millinewtons.

### 3.2 Maximum bow force

**Shape and censoring (post hoc).** Pooled over the 11 diagrams, the upper boundary scaled as v^0.70 [0.63, 0.76] β^−1.07 [−1.14, −1.01] (diagram-cluster bootstrap; with β-position clusters the β interval is [−1.14, −1.00]). The 4-N ceiling right-censors 25–42 %, 50–68 % and 56–83 % of available columns at 0.05, 0.10 and 0.20 m/s; the dataset authors did not compute the upper limit at 0.20 m/s [8]. Level fits therefore rely on the parametric shape in censored columns.

**A_T2 (frozen secondary endpoints).** Relative to the A1+A2 law the upper level rose by +0.197 [+0.106, +0.286] (A1−A2 yardstick 0.005), a Z exponent of +1.71 [+0.92, +2.49] (Schelleng: 1). Column-median errors: free-exponent law with published Z 0.121, lookup 0.167, gradient boosting 0.176 (pre-correction code), same law without Z 0.189; the frozen law's level error was 0.082. The primary hypothesis was rejected because it failed on the lower boundary (Sec. 3.3).

**D_T2 (frozen primary endpoint).** The strict law F = cZv/β with ln c = 0.966 from the five T1 diagrams predicted the D_T2 upper level within 0.002 (conditional interval [−0.078, 0.091]; tolerance 0.12) — passed. This is a censoring-weighted average over speeds of a shape that misfits D_T2's speed dependence: median signed column residuals were +0.07 (0.05 m/s, n 23), −0.01 (0.10, n 15) and −0.55 (0.20, n 10), with fitted σ 0.396. Column medians were 0.124 (strict), 0.113 (empirical shape + Z, frozen secondary) and 0.226 (no Z, uncalibrated); the strict law's column errors on the other T2 diagrams were 0.113–0.223. The test had little leverage on Z: the bootstrap level interval maps to Z exponents 0.37–1.74, a constant T2 offset without Z (post hoc) predicts D_T2 within 0.010, and D1 lay 0.094 above the strict T1 line while D_T2 lay on it. The result is a cross-type calibration success of the T1 constant, not a within-type Z¹ shift.

**D2 (frozen).** For a second D_T1 sample (≤4 N), all ten frozen upper laws were inside their 90 % intervals (strict cZv/β −0.126 against ±0.148; thermal −0.127; empirical shape −0.114; 109 columns, 60 finite, 49 right-censored); the β exponent was −1.13 [−1.21, −1.07] ("steep" zone, decidable) and the speed exponent 0.87 [0.79, 0.96].

**A4 and B_T2 sample 2 (frozen).** For a fourth A_T1 sample the strict law over-predicted the level (−0.173, outside ±0.148; only the zero-parameter thermal variant, with its wide ±0.41 interval, was inside); β exponent −1.12 [−1.30, −0.96]. For B_T2 sample 2 — four speeds including 0.15 m/s, 40 × 40 strokes, forces up to ≈7.5 N; scored with a dedicated parser frozen before its labels were read; partially contaminated because a friction-reconstruction session of the same date had been seen — the strict law passed narrowly (+0.129; +0.145 on the three standard speeds) while all thermal variants failed (+0.16 to +0.22); β exponent −0.98 [−1.20, −0.81], speed exponent 0.75 [0.60, 0.88].

**FROZEN_v3 pooled hypotheses (A4, D2, B_T2 sample 2).** The pooled speed curvature e(0.10→0.20) − e(0.05→0.10) was −0.11 (cluster-bootstrap interval [−0.30, +0.015]) and the lv·lb interaction +0.29 ([−0.09, +0.39]); neither the empirical (concave, positive interaction) nor the thermal hypotheses were decided. Per-diagram curvatures (−0.30, +0.01, −0.01) all lay inside the frozen single-diagram 90 % interval (−0.69, +0.06).

**Summary of all frozen tests of cZv/β (Table 3, Fig. 5).** Level errors over nine held-out sessions were −0.002 (D_T2), −0.173 (A4), −0.126 (D2), −0.066 (A3, frozen under FROZEN_v1 while it was being processed), +0.129 (B_T2 s2) and +0.165 to +0.364 (four high-force sessions); 4/9 passed and the SD was 0.20, larger than the in-sample residual SD (0.08–0.12) used to set the intervals. **Tension pairs (post hoc).** Z exponents of the T1→T2 pairs were A +1.71 [0.86, 2.47], B −0.51 [−1.79, 0.75], C +3.25 [1.11, 5.12] and D +0.23 [−0.25, 0.70]. A common exponent of 1 is rejected for A, B, C (χ² = 14.2, 3 df, p = 0.003). A random-effects estimate over A, B, C is +1.37 [−0.49, 3.23] (normal) or [−2.7, 5.5] (t, 2 df); the between-pair τ ≈ 0.12 matches the T1 strict-law residual SD (0.118). Three further limits apply. Z is exactly proportional to tension in this dataset (T/Z = 137.2 m/s for all eight strings), so Z, tension and the T2 batch are collinear. Across strings EI is associated with the upper level at least as strongly as Z (r 0.80 vs 0.71). The published C_T2 Z (1.1154 ± 0.1023 kg/s) has an uncertainty larger than the C pair's Δln Z (0.058), so the C exponent is not identified.

**High-force sessions (frozen, scored once).** All ten frozen upper-boundary models under-predicted the 4–12 N boundary of all four new sessions (Table 2, Fig. 4). For strict cZv/β the level errors (observed − predicted) were +0.36 (C), +0.22 (A), +0.17 (D) and +0.36 (B), all outside the frozen 90 % interval (±0.15). The free β exponents were −0.75 [−1.27, −0.44] (C), −0.82 [−1.30, −0.47] (A), −0.48 [−0.79, −0.24] (D) and −0.39 [−0.68, −0.16] (B). The frozen rule labels D and B "thermal zone" (e_β ≥ −0.95), but their slopes are far shallower than every frozen model, including the thermal ones (−0.86 to −0.94), so we read this only as "shallower than β^−1". Speed exponents were 0.57–0.91. The frozen speed-curvature test was not decided (pooled −0.19, cluster-bootstrap interval [−0.46, +0.17]; per string A +0.13, C +0.21, B −0.53, D −0.44). Pre-declared sensitivity (no-band columns, 9–13 per string, treated as F_max < lowest force) changed none of the decisions except that the curvature interval excluded 0 under one of the two pre-declared bootstraps; by the frozen rule this does not upgrade the result. Post hoc, the upper estimate in this range is fragile to how single-stroke label dropouts are handled: replacing the last-band rule by the longest-band rule moves the strict-law errors to +0.02, −0.14, −0.08 and +0.19. Columns with ≥ 2 separated H bands are not specific to high force (37–73 % of banded columns in the development diagrams, 47–70 % here; median gap one stroke). Session, force range and extraction rule are confounded, so the cause of the failure is not identified; the frozen verdict stands.

### 3.3 Minimum bow force

**Levels and repeatability (post hoc).** Lower-boundary levels varied across the ten development diagrams with SD 0.218. A1 and A2 differed by 0.233. The B_T2 same-day repeats differed by 0.18, but their intervals overlap ([−0.485, −0.183] vs [−0.269, −0.024]). In a variance decomposition the within-repeat SD was 0.121 (3 df) against 0.211 for condition means (F = 4.40, permutation p = 0.096). On the onset-force axis the between-diagram SD falls to 0.125.

**Per-diagram shape (post hoc).** Free per-diagram fits gave lower-boundary exponents v^1.09–1.42 and β^−1.61 to −2.18 for the eight non-A_T1 diagrams, against v^0.67/0.94 and β^−0.97/−1.09 for A1/A2. The A1+A2 reference shape used for all levels is therefore atypical; outside it the shape resembles Schelleng's v/β² in direction, with speed still confounded with session.

**Association with the session descriptor (post hoc).** The ten levels correlated with dec5_pl (range −0.120 to −0.032 N): r = 0.88 (Fisher interval about 0.56–0.97; nominal p = 0.0008, ×10 screen 0.008). The association is fragile:

- *Leverage.* Without A1, r = 0.77; without A1 and A2 (which define the reference shape), 0.69 (p 0.06), while EI gives +0.86 (p 0.01) and torsional stiffness +0.85 in the same eight diagrams. On levels taken at a fixed reference point (v = β = 0.1) of per-diagram free fits, r = 0.90 for the ten diagrams but 0.60 (p 0.12) for the eight.
- *Force axis.* Measured-force K/t variants give 0.815–0.880; the nominal axis 0.675–0.707. Reading boundaries on a decaying channel couples the descriptor and the boundary arithmetically.
- *Detection limit.* Treating readings below c as left-censored gives r = 0.80 (c = 0.2 N) and 0.70 (c = 0.3 N).
- *Attack.* A1, the extreme point, is the reverse-scanned diagram whose low-force strokes had 15× harsher attacks at 0.05 m/s; its higher level at 0.05 m/s (+0.24 vs A2 −0.25; +0.04/+0.05 vs −0.05/−0.04 at 0.10/0.20 m/s) has the speed profile expected from harsher attacks needing more force to establish Helmholtz motion [6]. Attack scheduling can explain at most the A1 offset, since the other diagrams share one schedule.

Refitting the reference shape inside each fold, the descriptor reduced the level error from 0.213 to 0.101 (leave-one-diagram-out) and from 0.221 to 0.131 (leave-one-type-out), but increased the finite-column error from 0.294 to 0.316 and from 0.319 to 0.354 (843 of 1200 columns finite); the censoring-inclusive interval distance changed from 0.315 to 0.303. Descriptor selection was outside the cross-validation loop, so these are optimistic.

**A_T2 (frozen secondary).** The lower level was 0.539 [0.382, 0.722] below the A1+A2 law (42 % lower; −0.65 relative to A1, −0.42 relative to A2; A1−A2 yardstick 0.233, i.e. ±2.0 in Z-exponent units). The implied Z exponent was −4.69 [−6.27, −3.32]. Treating readings below c as left-censored with the reference refitted the same way gives −0.42 (c = 0.2 N) and −0.29 (0.3 N); on the onset-force axis −0.19. The draft-v1 value −0.10 truncated on the outcome and is withdrawn. A_T2 also had the most negative dec5_pl (−0.120 N); this is an observation, not evidence that the session caused the shift.

**D_T2 (frozen).** The primary level fit failed because one column had a zero-width interval at the 1-mN floor, so the primary endpoint is **untestable**; the lower bootstrap failed in 1272 of 2000 resamples for the same reason. Secondary finite-column errors (111 columns) favoured the descriptor numerically (0.286, against 0.328 for the mean, 0.325 log EI, 0.376 log Z, 0.386 log Z + log EI), but the predeclared level-scale margin does not define a decision for this secondary metric. The planned K = 3, t = 0.1/0.3 s and nominal-axis secondaries were not run. D_T2's descriptor (−0.038 N) sat next to A1, the most influential training point; post hoc repairs of the zero-width column gave levels −0.09 to −0.13, errors 0.17–0.20 for the descriptor model and 0.17–0.21 for the mean.

**A4 (frozen).** A protocol-aware lower-boundary estimator (PACE*) was scored once against frozen baselines (120 columns, 85 finite, 8 no-band). It beat the clipped lookup on interval distance (0.165 vs 0.207; difference −0.042) and mean log score (−2.003 vs −2.225), so H1 and H1b were met; it was worse on finite-column error (0.241 vs 0.172; H2 failed), and the session/protocol terms added nothing (H3). A mean-only model was better than PACE* on all three metrics (0.204, 0.157, −1.961). The lookup failed to predict 3 columns, so its interval and log-score denominators are 117 against 120. We do not read this as a method advantage.

### 3.7 Session variance and string settling

**Variance decomposition (post hoc; partly refuted by an independent check).** For the 13 sessions recorded with the ≤4 N design (A1–A4, B1, C1, D1, D2, A_T2, B_T2s1, B_T2 Part 6, C_T2, D_T2; A3 scored once under a freeze written while it was being processed: strict level −0.066, inside its frozen interval), a REML model with known level SEs gave, for the upper level relative to strict cZv/β, a string type × tension component τ = 0.058 [0, 0.145] and a within-type session component ω = 0.069 [0.04, 0.13]; for the lower level τ = 0.114 [0, 0.32] and ω = 0.158 [0.09, 0.30]. An independent re-implementation reproduced every number but showed that the design cannot rank the two components: only five within-type degrees of freedom exist (posterior P(ω ≥ τ) = 0.6–0.7); the upper ω comes from the single D1–D2 pair and the lower ω from A1 (reverse scan) and A4; without dividing by the published Z, the type component is larger (τ = 0.10 [0.03, 0.22]); and β-block bootstrap SEs are 1.5–3 times larger. The supported statement is narrower: within some types (D_T1 upper; A_T1 lower, where the levels also track scan protocol, force floor and date), sessions differ as much as impedance-normalised types. Session repeatability has been examined before [2, 7, 17, 18]; the heterogeneous T1→T2 pair exponents are what such session differences would produce.

**Settling (frozen, one string).** The collection contains the A1 string recorded at 0.10 m/s immediately after mounting ("cold", 03-25) and after a play-in of several thousand strokes ("warm", 03-26), before the A1 diagram (03-27). A prediction was frozen while this archive was being processed (the cold folder's features already existed but were unopened; thresholds = ω/2). The lowest settings also delivered about three times more force in cold (median onset 0.48 vs 0.17 N): the elevation-to-force mapping changed as the string settled. We therefore report a probable upward shift of the minimum-force boundary during play-in of roughly 0.1–0.3, confounded with the force mapping, and no detectable change of the maximum force. A remount effect on transient playability [18] and a lower minimum force in a diagram repeated five months later [2] were reported before; a controlled new-versus-played-in comparison of steady-state limits was not found.

### 3.8 The minimum-force law across sessions (post hoc)

Per-session interval-censored fits log F_min = a + b_v log v + b_β log β (t = 0.2 s, K = 2) did not reproduce the development pattern in the four sessions never used for this statistic (window-force β: A3 −1.22, A4 −1.42, D2 −1.11, B_T2 s2 −1.31; development non-A_T1 −1.6 to −2.2), and the same-day repeat pair differed by 0.36, so a single diagram cannot separate β^−1 from β^−2. Replacing the window force by the label-free force at bow onset (11–12 sessions with ramp metadata) gave F_min ∝ v^0.95 [0.86, 1.05] β^−1.22 [−1.36, −1.09] (random effects, HKSJ), between-session SD 0.13/0.18; on the window-force axis the same sessions gave v^1.04–1.09 β^−1.45 to −1.54 with twice the scatter, and the onset-minus-window difference was +0.21 [+0.11, +0.32] in β. An independent check confirmed the stroke mapping, gave the same onset-axis result with β-block bootstrap (−1.26 [−1.39, −1.12] for blocks of 10), showed that a nominal-command axis is as scattered as the window axis, and found censoring variants moving single sessions between −0.9 and −1.6 but none near −2. We therefore report a sensitivity, not a law: in the same measured sessions, with the vibration state fixed at the same window, changing only the time at which the force coordinate is read (bow onset vs the analysis window) moves the minimum-force β exponent by ≈0.2 and halves its between-session scatter. The onset-referenced boundary is a command-to-outcome map, not a maintenance-stability limit in the current force, so the two coordinates do not estimate the same quantity and neither is assumed correct. A v/β law has already been proposed from simulations, together with a dependence of the fitted exponent on the analysis window [4]; the travel-dependent load decrease is known and the window-averaged force was used for that reason in the same collection [10]; establishment and maintenance thresholds differ in bowing-machine measurements [7]; and Schoonderwaldt et al. found measured lower-limit slopes not different from −2 [2]. A label-free transform of each session's force record (window force ∝ onset force^(1+c) with 1 + c = 1.12–1.63, i.e. relatively larger load loss at low force) predicted which sessions' exponents changed most between the two coordinates (correlation of predicted and observed β shift 0.78, speed shift 0.70) and the doubled scatter, but overstated the mean β shift (−0.33 vs −0.24), failing our pre-declared criterion; it is a partial protocol explanation. For Schoonderwaldt's modified law F = A(v + βz₀)/β², the local exponents satisfy p_β = −1 − p_v, so an observed p_v ≈ 1 with p_β ≈ −1.2 is not its low-speed limit. The onset axis was chosen after seeing the data and speed is confounded with recording block. Within-scan β and recording time are confounded in ascending scans; comparing the one descending scan (A1) with the other A T1 samples bounds the time-order bias of the β exponent at −0.06 ± 0.06 (onset) and −0.12 ± 0.10 (window), in the direction of a steeper, not shallower, exponent (assumes A1 shares the A T1 exponent; one reversed session). By its pre-registered reading the exponent is label-dependent (`novelty/label_sensitivity/`: range of variant means S = 2.4 on the window axis and 1.9 on the onset axis, against a pre-declared "label-dependent" band of S ≥ 0.3). Rebuilding the labels from the stored window features reproduced every stored session exponent exactly (13 window and 11 onset sessions), and an independent re-implementation reproduced the sweep. The periodicity threshold (0.7–0.9) moved the mean by ≤ 0.02; the single-flyback criterion (second flyback below 0.3–0.7 of the first) moved it by −0.17 to +0.17, a span of 0.34 on the window axis and 0.17 on the onset axis; post hoc, amplitude requirements scaled to the column median Helmholtz amplitude (or to v/β) moved it by ≤ +0.16; no such variant brought the mean near −2 (steepest −1.70, 95 % CI −1.89 to −1.51). The range statistic is dominated by a fixed amplitude floor, which moved the mean to −0.1 or +0.7 because it removes 42–58 % of Helmholtz windows in the largest-β tercile but 0–6 % in the smallest (measured Helmholtz amplitude ∝ v^0.9 β^−0.75); this confound was documented in the classifier before the analysis but not flagged in the plan. Without it the range is 0.34 (window) and 0.17 (onset), still "label-dependent" and "moderate" by the pre-declared bands. The conclusion that the measured minimum force falls more gently than β^−2 therefore survives every β-neutral threshold tested, but the value of the exponent is threshold-sensitive at the ±0.17 level, and at large β many columns are left-censored at the lowest force tested. A sustained-motion requirement, the label kind that mattered most in the simulations, changed nothing: requiring the stroke to stay Helmholtz (or keep ≥ 90 % of its amplitude) until 0.8 s (0.3 s at 0.2 m/s) shifted the mean exponent by −0.001 to +0.03 on both axes, because 92–100 % of measured Helmholtz strokes at 0.2 s persist; for the one session whose raw archive is on disk (B1, streamed without extraction; recomputed features matched the stored ones for 6000/6000 strokes), corner-width labels from the bridge-force flyback duration shifted the exponent by −0.06 to +0.22 unless the threshold was tied to the bow–bridge round trip βT0: requiring a corner no wider than βT0 steepened B1 from −1.66 to −2.53, but this variant is β-confounded, because the measured corner width scales as β^0.48 (median 0.99 βT0) and the threshold removes 97 % of Helmholtz strokes at small β against 19 % at large β. The same mechanism — a β-scaled acceptance threshold applied to a quantity that does not scale with β — plausibly explains the steep exponents of the simulations' amplitude/slip label (post hoc).

### 3.9 String damping and the absolute minimum force (post hoc, zero fitted parameters)

On this rigid-termination monochord, Schelleng's minimum force F_S = Z²v/[2Rβ²(μs − μd)] [1] can be filled entirely from independent measurements on the same rig: Z from the dataset paper [8], the friction jump μs − μd1 ≈ 0.6 at low force [17], and the loss from the plucked-string modal damping factors ζ_n of each string [8, Fig. 1], which we digitized (per-panel grid-line calibration, residual ≤ 0.24 px) and converted to an energy-weighted equivalent resistance Z/R_eff = (6/π)Σ_{n≤10} ζ_n/n (Helmholtz-motion mode energies ∝ 1/n²; reduces to Z/R = πζ₁ for a frequency-independent R). The plan, constants and reading bands were fixed before the damping values were extracted (`novelty/string_damping/README.md`). At v = 0.1 m/s, β = 0.1 the prediction is 0.020–0.044 N, while the measured boundary is 0.31–0.50 N on the onset axis and 0.16–0.29 N on the window axis: Q = F_meas/F_S = 8.7–20 (onset, all seven types above the pre-declared "order of magnitude" threshold of 8) and 4.6–14 (window); the prediction band over N, the derivation constant and Δμ ∈ [0.6, 1] lowers Q to 5–10 at most. Because the measured β exponent is −1.2 to −1.5 rather than −2, the excess grows with β (onset Q ≈ 2.5–6 at β = 0.02 and 15–34 at β = 0.2). In every (speed, β) column in which a non-Helmholtz stroke was observed, its force exceeded F_S (onset: 1,216 columns, median ratio 8.0, > 4 × F_S in 91 %), and the failure was not only an attack effect: among slowly accelerated strokes (< 0.15 m/s²) 57 % were not Helmholtz at 4–8 × F_S, against 88–99 % for faster attacks (acceleration is confounded with speed and recording order). Across string types the damping did not order the minimum force: the slope of the type level on log(Z·Σζ_n/n) was +0.21 [−0.45, +0.87] on the onset axis (prediction +1; pre-declared verdict "contradicted") and +0.08 [−0.90, +1.06] on the window axis, with no variant (N = 5, 15, ζ₁ only) excluding zero; same-type sessions, however, differed as much as types did (within-type SD 0.16 vs between-type 0.16, onset), so this test has little power. The absolute result replicates Schoonderwaldt et al., who found Schelleng's minimum with pluck-derived R too low by a factor 6–11 on a violin D/E string and a monochord [2]; with their fundamental-only convention our cello ratios are 14–50 at β = 0.1. They also found c_lower ∝ R^−2.3 when damping was added to one string [2]; across our string types no such dependence appeared. A pre-registered simulation programme (corner-exact digital waveguide with the A T1 parameters; eight versions, all plans committed before running and all failures kept in `novelty/sim_minforce/`: three failed their own validation or filter gates, and a finite-width contact model failed even at 0.7 mm width, an artifact) reproduced Schelleng for frequency-independent loss with both a Coulomb and a smooth friction law (β exponent −2.0 to −2.05). With the measured, frequency-dependent string damping, however, every quantity depended on the friction law and on the definition of sustained Helmholtz motion: at β = 0.1 the damping changed the maintenance minimum by a factor between 0.4 and 27, the β exponent ranged from −2.7 to −6.5 among labels requiring sustained motion (a label accepting decaying motion was censored by the force grid), no version gave the measured flattening (−1.2 to −1.5) or level robustly, and none established Helmholtz motion from rest under the robot's slow ramp. The simulation therefore does not identify the cause of the shortfall; it shows that, in this model class, the answer is not robust to the friction law or to the Helmholtz criterion, echoing the classification grey zone near the minimum force noted by Schoonderwaldt et al. [2] (post hoc; not independently verified).

### 3.10 Empirical criterion-tilt responses (locally planned tests on exposed data)

The amplitude-floor and corner-width observations motivated a fixed-removal experiment (`novelty/tilt_principle/`). For a feature threshold X ≥ Kβ^a, K was chosen within each speed folder to remove approximately 15% of base-H windows, and a was varied over a fixed grid. The original amplitude results over 14 diagrams rose monotonically with a: mean exponent shifts were −0.65, −0.27, +0.14, +0.35, +0.60, +0.75 and +0.85 for a = −1.5 … 0. The neutral point was −1.09, compared with the marginal amplitude slope −0.92; these were within the declared tolerance of 0.25. Height behaved similarly. The tests used already exposed diagrams: freezing the analysis before execution does not constitute validation on new data.

### 3.11 Force ordering is necessary for quantitative criterion-response modelling (post hoc)

**Identifiability.** For exactly m removals of the smallest log X − a log β, the sum of log β among removed windows is nondecreasing with a (the two optimality inequalities for the selected sets give this result). This rank property does not imply monotonicity of a boundary exponent. A finite-grid example using the actual K=2 boundary definition and a nondegenerate interval-normal fit gives an exponent change 0 → −0.11905 when a increases 0 → 1, despite the expected rank shift. A second example has a = b = 0 but moves the exponent 0 → +1. Permuting its features within one β column preserves the entire feature distribution in that column, the marginal slope, β and force grids, base labels, removal fraction and removed β columns, yet changes the exponent shift from +1 to 0. Consequently those marginal summaries cannot uniquely determine the fitted boundary response. The joint ordering of feature and force is indispensable (`theory/REPORT.md`, `counterexamples.py`).

**A sufficient model, not a universal formula.** Let x = log β, log F₀ = c + px, u = log F − log F₀, and log X = α + bx + qu + σε for Gaussian ε and q > 0. For the lower cutoff log X ≥ α + k(a) + ax, the additional operational force required for retention probability r is u_r = max{0, [k(a) + (a−b)x − σΦ⁻¹(1−r)]/q}. When this cutoff is active in every column and q and σ are constant, the slope shift is (a−b)/q. Here b is conditional on relative force; it is generally not the marginal H-feature slope. The formula concerns a probability boundary and is not an exact formula for the first observed K-run. Partial activation, finite grids, correlated labels and censoring require additional treatment.

**Conditional replay.** We fixed the retrospective model, feature grids, 20 Gaussian replications per feature and diagram, seed and scoring thresholds before computing the new outputs (`novelty/criterion_identifiability/PLAN.md`). M0 regresses log feature on speed-folder intercepts and log β among base-H windows; M1 adds log window force. Neither model fits the observed exponent shifts. Both regenerate features on the original H, β and force grid, remove approximately 15% using the original quantile rule, and apply the same K=2 extraction followed by the stable interval likelihood. Four features over 13 diagrams contribute 338 diagram–tilt cells; B1 corner width is a separate auxiliary analysis. These are correlated conditions, not 338 independent experiments, and not all 13 diagrams are independent specimens.

**Accuracy and audits.** Against the original stored curves, MAE was 0.13267 for M0 and 0.07720 for M1. Four nonpositive-force H windows were ineligible only for M1; repeating M0 with the identical mask gave 0.13248 → 0.07720 (41.73% reduction). Stable refits of the observed curves exposed four legacy optimization discrepancies exceeding 0.02 in the β exponent (maximum 0.1997). With both matched masks and those stable targets, MAE was 0.13365 → 0.07708 (42.32%). The declared gates—overall M1 MAE ≤0.15, reduction ≥25%, and each feature MAE ≤0.25—passed in each audit. Main replay fits numbered 13,720 (219 numerical fallbacks, zero unresolved failures); the matched-mask sensitivity added 1,660 fits with zero failures. Baseline parity was within 1.31×10⁻⁵. These checks validate the computation, not the physical model.

**Limits and failures.** For amplitude, the mean neutral-point prediction was −1.0933 versus −1.0881 observed. However, M1 predicted a second-flyback-ratio neutral point at +0.8325 where the observed curve never crossed zero. Periodicity improved slightly against stable targets but worsened slightly against the legacy targets; there is no across-feature dominance. The Gaussian model can generate features outside base-H acceptance conditions (expected M1 session-average rates: periodicity outside [0.8,1], 0.054%; ratio ≥0.5, 2.337%). This is a diagnostic surrogate, not a valid joint generator of H waveforms, and these tail violations have not been established as the cause of the ratio failure. The plotted uncertainty is Monte Carlo error at fixed data and fitted coefficients, not a population interval. All replay accuracy is internal to the observed base labels and grids; new-session prediction and causal explanation are untested.

**Endpoint locality.** Using the same 13 diagrams, we counted how the original threshold changes affect the first and last K-runs, without fitting new laws. Across amplitude tilts, 43.6% of originally banded column–tilt pairs changed their lower endpoint, compared with 5.9% at the upper endpoint; 5.7% lost all runs. For periodicity, 16.6% lost all runs. Counts reuse columns across tilts. This locates the discrete pathway by which similar removal fractions affect the boundaries differently; it does not prove the sign of a fitted upper exponent. It also shows why an assumed right-censoring direction for no-band columns can matter. The relevant method contribution is to audit criterion response through feature–force dependence and boundary extraction, with the mathematical and empirical limits made explicit.

### 3.12 Exact label changes needed to reverse a boundary-law compatibility decision (post hoc)

**Decision and estimand.** Before computing the new edit costs, we fixed `novelty/run_fragility/PLAN.md` in local commit `37c1a46`. Prior exponent results and all empirical datasets had already been seen; this is not independent confirmation. For each single-speed folder, fix a slope p and allow a free intercept a in log F = a + p log beta. A column's first K force-adjacent H labels define a finite log-force bracket [l,u]. The new decision is whether one a is compatible with **every eligible bracket**, expanded by a declared tolerance epsilon. Primary settings are K=2 and epsilon=.2; p=−2 is the classical minimum-force shape, whereas p=−1.2 is a descriptive reference selected from earlier empirical estimates, not the exact −1 law of [4]. Epsilon is an operational resolution, not a confidence interval or measured noise level. This all-column predicate is different from the earlier interval-MLE inference.

**Exact construction.** A trailing-run-state dynamic program gives the minimum allowed Hamming cost C_js for every possible first-run start s in column j in O(nK) time. It specializes established minimum correction to a regular language [22]. Each candidate bracket becomes an intercept interval I_js=[l_js−p log beta_j−epsilon, u_js−p log beta_j+epsilon]. The exact compatibility-restoration distance is min_a sum_j min_{s:a in I_js} C_js. A sweep of interval endpoints and open cells finds this minimum without an intercept grid. For the opposite direction, a family of one-dimensional closed intervals has empty intersection exactly when two intervals from different columns are strictly disjoint. Minimizing their incremental costs above per-column minima gives the exact exclusion distance. Both bounds are attained by reconstructed local label edits. The direction used for reversal depends on the original verdict. A distance d certifies that fewer than d permitted edits cannot reverse that verdict. General fragility indices and restrictions on admissible changes have prior art [23]; our object is the discrete boundary-to-law pipeline, not a new generic fragility concept.

**Support and validation.** Membership is fixed from the original labels: 1,132 of 1,560 columns (56,600 of 78,000 windows) have finite positive K=2 lower brackets. We exclude 315 originally left-censored columns and 113 no-run columns. Edited states must also have finite positive brackets; a column cannot disappear. This prevents trivial all-censored escapes but restricts the estimand to the originally resolved subset. Independent enumeration checked 92,238 local endpoint/no-run costs, 3,016 global fixtures (218,318 candidate combinations), and 540 end-to-end cases (107,742 label combinations), all with agreement. Independent reapplication checked 355 feasible primary witnesses and 4,901 listed edits across the 234 directional records. Combinatorial exactness is conditional on ordinary floating-point endpoint comparisons, not exact physical measurement. All 53 unrestricted primary restoration optima have positive-width common-intercept intersections and remain attainable under open-lower brackets.

**Empirical results.** Under the primary all-column rule, both slopes are initially incompatible in all 39 speed folders. For p=−2, compatibility can be restored in 14 folders by 2–20 edits (median 13.5). The median eligible-window edit fraction is 0.884%, while the median changed-beta-column fraction is 38.32%. For p=−1.2, all 39 folders permit restoration, with 5–163 edits (median 26), 1.68% of eligible windows and 48.28% of columns at the medians. These are separate sets; comparing their unpaired medians as model selection would be invalid. Even on the common 14 folders, where −2 costs less in 10 cases (medians 13.5 versus 20), 11 folders are at the highest speed and all retain only 13–32 of 40 original columns. Minimum edit cost is not a calibrated physical model-selection score.

**Geometry versus sensitivity.** The remaining 25 p=−2 cases are force-grid infeasible under the stated finite-boundary restriction, not infinitely robust physical rejections. Ignoring costs, each column's candidate intercept intervals form a contiguous union. If L_j and U_j are its endpoints before tolerance expansion, feasibility requires max_j L_j−epsilon <= min_j U_j+epsilon. Thus the post hoc diagnostic epsilon_grid=max(0,(max_j L_j−min_j U_j)/2) supplies an obstruction certificate from two columns. It agrees with all 78 primary slope-folder geometry decisions. It is not valid as a sufficient condition for disconnected candidate unions. At epsilon=0,.1,.2,.4 the numbers of p=−2 restoration-feasible folders are 12,13,14,20; all directional and K sensitivities are retained, including initially compatible cases where the reversal distance is the exclusion cost, not zero.

**Interpretation.** Sparse arbitrary window edits may affect many beta boundaries. They do not establish actual misclassification, and need not be realizable by a single coherent feature-based classifier. The unrestricted edit distance is a lower bound for that more restricted problem. A hypothetical witness demonstrates sensitivity, not corrected truth. The benefit over an average criterion-response error is an exact, auditable decision guarantee for a precisely stated target, together with a separate support diagnosis. No original confidence interval, frozen score, force measurement or physical claim is replaced. Input fixtures, proofs, results, independent verifiers and figures are in `novelty/run_fragility/`.

### 3.13 Shared waveform thresholds sharply restrict attainable reversals (post hoc)

**Declared rule families.** The previous certificate permits independent label substitutions. We now change one final waveform-feature gate at a time, applying one absolute scalar threshold to every window in all 39 folders. The primary ranges replace the periodicity floor .8 by t in [.7,.9] or the second-flyback-ratio ceiling .5 by t in [.3,.7]. Separate uncalibrated stress families add amplitude or flyback-height floors t in [0,+infinity] to the original H rule. Additional sensitivities extend periodicity and ratio to [0,1]. Other gates and waveform extraction remain fixed; force, beta, speed and session identity are not classifier inputs. Nonfinite scores are locked to their original labels. All four nominal rules reproduce the previous 78,000 labels exactly. The original finite-positive K=2 eligible columns, slopes and epsilon=.2 remain those of §3.12. The rule is also applied to originally excluded columns, whose changes count toward the all-data cost. A folder with any newly unresolved eligible boundary cannot count as compatible. The protocol was committed locally before calculating these costs (`e94d8e4`), on already exposed data.

**Complete computation and reachable boundaries.** Sorted threshold sweeps are established [24]; every tied score group must change together. Between consecutive scores, the labels and their downstream boundaries are constant. Enumerating the complete threshold arrangement therefore gives exact costs and attainable decisions for the declared families, without observation-specific edits. A structural diagnostic was specified before costs (`61b5304`): for H_i(t)=fixed_i OR (guard_i AND score_i>=t), let q_s be the minimum effective score in K adjacent force-ranked windows. The first run starts at s exactly when max_{r<s} q_r < t <= q_s. Only strict record-high q_s values can be first boundaries. A strict upper-score gate gives the dual record-low condition on K-window maxima. Fixed labels and infinite endpoints are treated explicitly. This elementary order-statistic reduction is independently implemented; it does not introduce record statistics. Even individually reachable column boundaries can require conflicting common thresholds.

**Attainability results.** At epsilon=.2, the periodicity family restores neither slope in any folder. The ratio family restores −2 only in B_T2r1 at 0.10 m/s. The amplitude and height stress families restore −1.2 only in B_T2r1 at 0.05 m/s, the same folder for both features. Extending periodicity and ratio to [0,1] adds no primary-tolerance success. Thus the union across all examined families attains 1/14 geometrically feasible −2 folders, and 1/39 for −1.2. The 25 already geometry-infeasible −2 folders add no evidence about classifier restrictions. The two successful speed folders belong to the same setup repeat, not independent replication across string types.

**Exact costs and collateral changes.** The ratio rule's successful primary range is [.3,.33218555281080747], a positive-width interval. Choosing its least-cost pattern changes 44 of the target folder's 1,200 eligible-window labels across 21/24 eligible beta columns. The arbitrary minimum is 5; because the coherent rule only deletes H labels, the direction-matched arbitrary minimum is 6. Changes total 88 within that complete folder, and 3,200 across all 78,000 windows (4.10%). Ten eligible boundaries in nine other folders become unresolved. Amplitude and height require respectively 32 and 26 changes in the same 650-window eligible target set, versus arbitrary minima 5 in both directions or 6 deletion-only. Applied globally, they change 12,021 and 11,492 labels, with boundary losses in 23 and 22 other folders. Within-target coherence costs and all-data collateral costs must be distinguished; dividing a global count by a local bound would conflate them. These hypothetical changes do not establish physical errors or damage.

**Structural and tolerance diagnostics.** In the primary periodicity range, 1,112/1,132 eligible columns have only one attainable finite first boundary. The median fraction of finite-force candidate starts that can be reached is 2.08% for periodicity, 6.25% for ratio, 20.83% for amplitude and 18.75% for height. This diagnoses a within-column restriction, not the probability of a correct label or the whole cross-column coherence gap. After inspecting collateral losses, a separate post hoc diagnostic required all 1,132 original eligible boundaries across all folders to remain finite-positive. Under that stricter condition no examined family restores any folder at epsilon=.2. This statement must remain tolerance-specific: at epsilon=.4, four −2 folders are already compatible, and the ratio rule adds A4 at 0.20 m/s using 150 all-data edits with no boundary loss. Six folders can each be made compatible using different ratio thresholds, but at most five simultaneously. Other families exchange the identity of compatible folders without increasing maximum coverage. All local success sets, identities and baseline counts are retained.

**Independent verification and scope.** A direct-classification oracle matched 828 synthetic cases, 34,620 profile comparisons and 43,236 minimum checks. The record-envelope API passed 94,024 endpoint/cell checks. For empirical validation, each column's local feature thresholds were classified afresh and mapped to every shared threshold, independently of the production event updates. All 182,057 shared-threshold patterns and 157,115,191 stored profile scalars agreed. The audit also checked 48 global optima, 288 budget optima and 1,872 folder optima, and reapplied 48 global threshold witnesses to all windows. These are computational checks, not independent scientific samples. Copy-only reproduction regenerates the complete profiles, verification and figures without raw archives.

The result is an exact rule-constrained sensitivity audit, not a classifier with validated physical accuracy. Multiverse analysis already addresses processing choices [25]; predictive multiplicity typically imposes predictive-error admissibility [26], which we cannot establish without independent true-motion labels. Our contribution is the verified gap between arbitrary edits and attainable waveform-rule changes, together with first-boundary reachability and consequences elsewhere. Neither scalar sweep novelty, a new physical minimum-force law, old-MLE reversal nor a certificate over unsearched multifeature classifiers is claimed.

## 4. Discussion

**What the collection supports.** (1) Measurement protocol enters the boundaries through at least four routes: travel-dependent force loss, an attack schedule tied to recording order (and so to force only for ascending scans), speed-as-session, and window placement. (2) A Z¹-scaled maximum-force law with one calibrated constant predicted the level of held-out sessions only within about ±0.2 (4 of 9 frozen tests inside their frozen intervals), with errors growing with the force ceiling; within-type tension pairs were heterogeneous; Z, tension, EI and the T2 batch cannot be separated here. (3) The between-diagram minimum-force variation is associated post hoc with a same-session load-decrease descriptor (surviving a screen-wide max-|r| correction), but the association weakens on the onset-force axis, under detection-limit censoring and without the reverse-scanned A1; a pure reading-drop mechanism is not ruled out; and frozen tests were untestable (D_T2) or favoured the lookup on boundary-level metrics (A4, D2). (4) For the minimum force of an unseen string, a lookup of an already-measured string was not beaten.

**Recommendations for users of this collection.**

- Report boundaries on both onset-force and window-force axes until the cause of the travel-dependent decrease is resolved.
- Record the scan direction and the attack of each stroke; treat each speed folder as a session.
- Align analysis windows by time since plateau onset *and* report bow travel.
- Report censored, no-band and multi-band columns separately; no-band is not a censoring direction.
- Include a model-free lookup baseline and state metric denominators.
- Report criterion-response curves and which first/last K-runs they alter; matching marginal feature scaling alone is insufficient.
- Distinguish arbitrary label-change bounds from changes achievable by an explicit shared classifier. Count edits and lost boundaries outside the target condition as well.

**Sessions and play-in.** Within some types, sessions differed as much as impedance-normalised types, and play-in probably moved one string's minimum-force boundary while also changing the elevation-to-force mapping. String comparisons in this collection — and plausibly with other elevation-controlled bowing machines — need replicate specimens or sessions with recorded play-in state and a force calibration per session, not only same-day repeats.

**What is and is not new.** Z scaling of the maximum force follows from Schelleng's analysis [1] and has been examined across strings [2,12,13]; establishment versus maintenance [6,7], thermal friction [10,14], and batch-effect adjustment [15,16] are known. The dataset group lists session effects qualitatively for attack measurements [17]. Within the literature we searched we did not find a quantitative audit of these protocol features in this collection, nor once-only frozen tests of boundary laws on its unseen diagrams and high-force sessions; the failed out-of-range transfer is, to our knowledge, new information about how far low-force Schelleng fits extend. The order-of-magnitude shortfall of Schelleng's minimum force with measured losses (§3.9) is not new [2]; its persistence on cello strings, for slow attacks and across eight string types is a replication. Regime-classification and analysis-window effects already have quantitative bowed-string prior art [4]. Covariate-adjusted thresholds [19], artificial scaling caused by detection thresholds [20], and interval-censored likelihood [21] are also established. We therefore claim neither the general threshold-bias phenomenon nor a new censoring estimator. The candidate contribution in §§3.10–3.11 is an application-specific account of feature–force ordering, discrete K-run extraction and criterion-response replay, together with counterexamples to simpler neutrality claims. Its novelty is bounded by the literature reviewed, and the replay is not a robustness certificate or independent prediction result. Section 3.12 adds a separate exact certificate for a declared finite-interval compatibility predicate. Regular-language correction [22], fragility [23] and one-dimensional interval geometry are established; the specific contribution combines these with first-run force boundaries, explicit witnesses, boundary-coverage reporting and support diagnostics. We have not established worldwide priority or a new physical law. Section 3.13 further shows that the small arbitrary-edit bounds are not generally realized by the examined waveform rules. It connects a record-envelope characterization of attainable first boundaries to exact shared-threshold decisions and global collateral effects. The value is a substantive qualification of label-fragility interpretation, not the invention of threshold sweeping.

**Friction.** In a public friction reconstruction of one string of uncertain identity (closest to B_T2) [14], the mean dynamic friction coefficient scaled as (F v)^−0.18; the friction contrast Δμ, which enters Schelleng's F_max, did not collapse on F v. The thermal-friction upper laws lost to plain Schelleng in all four high-force sessions. We therefore make no thermal-friction claim.

## 5. Limitations

- **Scope.** One robot, one monochord, cello G strings. Nothing here is a violin measurement; violin statements in the literature [2,3] are cited, not tested.
- **Small samples and confounding.** The original development cohort has 11 diagrams, with later diagrams and four high-force sessions added in separate analyses. Sections 3.11–3.13 use 13 diagrams. Speed, session, date, tension and scan direction are confounded; diagrams are not necessarily independent specimens.
- **Labels and forces.** Waveform-proxy labels; zero-offset uncertainty of tens of mN below 0.2 N; descriptor and boundaries share one force channel.
- **Physics predictors.** Losses enter only through §3.9, from damping factors digitized from a published figure (one pluck set per type, measured on sample 1; sessions A2–A4 and D2 used other samples). "String properties" in §3.2–3.4 means Z, EI, T, d and torsional stiffness only. Torsional losses, bow-hair compliance and ripple/corner rounding, which are candidates for the §3.9 excess [2,5], were not modelled.
- **Conventions.** K = 2 and the single-band, no-band-as-lower-right-censored convention are operational assumptions; the upper estimate at high force is sensitive to the band rule.
- **Tests.** Local freezes (one with an external commit timestamp), one diagram or session per test; the ML baselines are trained on several windows per stroke with random-row internal validation (pseudo-replication).
- **Coverage.** All applicable frozen predictions, including the cold/warm analysis, have been scored. PACE* on B_T2 sample 2 was declared inapplicable to its design. No unused confirmation set remains for §§3.11–3.13.
- **Criterion replay.** Same-session features and known base labels are used. Gaussian tails can violate base-H acceptance, and second-flyback-ratio neutrality is not reproduced. This does not establish physical label accuracy or new-string generalization.

- **Exact-edit scope.** Section 3.12 uses a retrospective all-column tolerance rule and originally finite positive boundaries. Its arbitrary independent label edits are not calibrated errors or necessarily achievable by a shared classifier. Support infeasibility and edit costs must not be ranked as physical evidence. It does not reverse the earlier MLE test.

- **Coherent-rule scope.** Section 3.13 searches one scalar final gate at a time. Independent accuracy, jointly varied feature thresholds, alternative waveform extraction and beta-dependent classifiers are not certified. Its no-boundary-loss finding is a post hoc condition at epsilon=.2 and fails to generalize to epsilon=.4.
