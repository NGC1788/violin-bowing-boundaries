## Abstract

Empirical bow-force laws estimated from robotic Schelleng diagrams depend on how bridge-force waveforms are labelled and how labelled strokes become force boundaries. In post hoc analyses of measured sessions of a public robotic cello-string collection, the minimum-force β exponent moved by about 0.2 when force was read at bow onset rather than in the analysis window, and by −0.17 to +0.17 under β-neutral changes of the single-flyback criterion, without approaching −2. We then audit, post hoc, 78,000 windows from 39 single-speed folders. For a declared decision (whether one law with fixed exponent p and free intercept fits every finite K = 2 lower boundary of a folder within log-force tolerance 0.2), arbitrary label edits can restore compatibility with p = −2 in 14 folders (exact minima 2–20 edits); the other 25 are force-grid infeasible. The examined shared waveform rules are far more restrictive: a complete search of 22,282,064 periodicity/ratio parameter cells restores p = −2 in one folder and p = −1.2 in none. Of 52 failures reversible by arbitrary edits, 50 are certified throughout the rule domain by two fixed β columns and two need exactly three. Retaining every originally finite boundary still permits one restoration at tolerance 0.205553, using 150 global label changes, near the declared cutoff. These geometric certificates are not likelihood-rejection certificates for the original interval-censored maximum-likelihood fit: deleting the selected certificate moved the fitted exponent away from the target in 35 of 52 cases, and all 24 minimum triples moved it away. We establish no true-motion labels, new physical law or reversal of earlier confidence intervals.

## 1. Introduction

Helmholtz motion of a bowed string occurs only between a minimum and a maximum bow force. Schelleng's analysis gives a maximum force proportional to Z v/β and a minimum force proportional to Z² v/(R β²) [1], with Z the characteristic impedance, v the bow speed, β the relative bow–bridge distance and R the termination resistance. Measurements and simulations disagree about the minimum force: it was nearly independent of bow speed on violin strings [2], had a non-zero baseline at very low speed [3], and scaled as Z v/β in a recent simulation preprint [4]. Theory describes the force needed to *sustain* Helmholtz motion [5], whereas a stroke starting from rest must first *establish* it [6,7].

Public robotic data make such laws testable across string types. A collection from the IWK (University of Music and Performing Arts Vienna) provides complete Schelleng diagrams for cello G strings of four constructions at two tensions, recorded by a bowing robot [8,9]; it has been used to validate a thermal friction model [10]. In such data a force boundary is never observed directly. Each analysis window of a stroke is first labelled by a bridge-force waveform classifier; a boundary is then read from the force-ordered labels with a run rule; and a law is fitted to the boundaries with an interval-censored likelihood [11]. A fitted exponent is therefore a property of the measurements, the classifier and the extraction rule together. Regime-classification and analysis-window effects already have quantitative bowed-string prior art [4], and Schoonderwaldt et al. noted a classification grey zone near the minimum force [2]. More generally, covariate-adjusted thresholds [12] and artificial scaling caused by detection thresholds [13] are established.

This paper asks a narrower question that can be answered exactly. For a stated law-compatibility decision, how many label changes are needed to reverse it? Which of those changes can be realized by one classifier rule shared across all data, and at what cost elsewhere? Which fixed measurement locations explain the decisions that no shared rule can reverse? And what do these geometric statements imply for the established interval-censored maximum-likelihood (MLE) fit? We contribute:

- **Measured motivation, with a correction.** In the measured sessions the minimum-force β exponent depends on the time at which the force coordinate is read and on the strictness of the single-flyback criterion. Counterexamples show that marginal feature scaling cannot determine how a fitted boundary responds to a criterion change; the joint ordering of feature and force is necessary (§3).
- **Exact label-edit sensitivity.** A first-K-run edit calculation combined with an intercept sweep gives the exact minimum number of label changes that reverses a declared all-column compatibility decision, with explicit witnesses, and separates force-grid infeasibility from edit cost (§4).
- **Shared-rule attainability and minimum obstructions.** Complete threshold-arrangement enumeration for scalar rules and a joint periodicity/ratio rule shows which arbitrary-edit reversals the declared shared rules can realize and with what collateral changes; fixed sets of two or three β columns certify impossibility throughout the rule domain; and a continuous tolerance diagnostic locates how close the declared cutoff lies to a transition (§5).
- **Non-implication for the original MLE.** Complete deletion enumeration, including the entire family of minimum certificates and a replay of coherent classifier rules, shows that geometric certificates are not likelihood-rejection certificates (§6).

*Scope.* All analyses are post hoc on data that had already been exposed. Where stated (§2.5), plans were committed locally before the corresponding costs were computed; other steps, including the onset-force axis (§3.1), the support diagnostic (§4.5), the no-loss condition of the scalar audit (§5.4) and the fixed-column obstructions (§5.6), were chosen after seeing results. None was publicly preregistered, and no unused confirmation set remains. We have no independent true-motion labels (none were found in the public metadata), so label edits are hypothetical sensitivity statements, not counts of misclassifications. We propose no new physical law and no new interval-censoring estimator, and we do not reverse earlier MLE confidence intervals or any frozen score. The generic ingredients are established (§7.1); the candidate contribution is their exact, verified composition with run-defined force boundaries. All measurements are of cello strings on one monochord; nothing here is a violin measurement. "log" denotes the natural logarithm throughout.

## 2. Data, labels and boundary estimation

### 2.1 Data

We use the public IWK collection [9] (CC BY 4.0). The dataset paper calls each 2000-stroke single-speed scan a "Schelleng diagram" [8]; here a *diagram* is the 6000-stroke set of one string sample over three bow speeds (0.05, 0.10, 0.20 m/s) × 40 log-spaced bow positions (β = 0.02–0.20) × 50 log-spaced nominal forces (0.1–4 N), sampled at 50 kHz. The criterion replay of §3.3 and Sections 4–6 use 13 diagrams: A1, A2, B1, C1 and D1 at the lower tension T1; A_T2, B_T2s1, C_T2 and the same-day repeats B_T2r1/r2 at the higher tension T2; and the further T1 samples A3, A4 and D2. Together these give 39 single-speed folders, 1,560 (speed, β) columns and 78,000 windows, one analysis window per stroke. D_T2 is excluded from these 13-diagram analyses (in the criterion replay, its 1 mN force clamp produces zero-width intervals). Sections 3.1–3.2 and the fixed-removal tilt experiment at the start of §3.3 (14 diagrams) use session sets with their own denominators, stated there.

The 13 diagrams reuse exposed data rather than adding independent observations; several were earlier frozen targets whose scores are unchanged (Supplement). We count diagrams, not independent strings: the two repeats share one setup, and speed is confounded with recording block because each speed is a separate complete β–force scan.

### 2.2 Regime labels

Each 0.2-s window is labelled by a fixed bridge-force waveform classifier (one flyback per period = "Helmholtz", H), with amplitude thresholds set once on A2. Its final gates require a periodicity score of at least 0.8 and a second flyback below 0.5 of the first (the "second-flyback ratio"), in addition to fixed physical gates. Force, β, speed and session identity are not classifier inputs. Primary windows start t = 0.2 s after the bow-speed plateau begins, and the force of a window is its mean measured bow force. The label is a proxy for string motion; forces below about 0.2 N are uncertain by tens of millinewtons.

### 2.3 Runs and boundary classes

Within each speed × β column the strokes are sorted by window force. A K-run consists of K H labels that are adjacent in this force order. Its members are different strokes, each represented by its window at the same time since plateau onset; they are not consecutive time windows of one stroke. With K = 2 (the dataset paper uses K = 3 [8]), a column's first K force-adjacent H labels define the lower boundary as a finite log-force bracket [l, u] immediately below the first run. Boundaries outside the measured range are censored, not imputed.

Three classes result. A column is *finite* when this bracket lies inside the measured range (1,132 of 1,560 columns; 56,600 of 78,000 windows), *left-censored* when the boundary lies below the lowest force tested (315 columns), and *no-run* when the column contains no run (113 columns). No-run columns are recorded as right-censored for the lower boundary. This is an *assumption*: with binary labels and the same error rate outside the band, an H region entirely below and entirely above the measured range give identical likelihoods, so the binary data cannot identify the censoring direction. Upper boundaries are not analysed here.

### 2.4 The established baseline: interval-censored MLE

The original estimator is retained unchanged. For each diagram it pools all 120 β columns across the three speeds and fits log F = c + a log v + b log β with Gaussian residual scale σ, scoring each row by the probability mass of its interval: a finite row by its bracket, a left-censored row by the lower tail and a no-run row by the upper tail. Interval-censored likelihood is established methodology [11], not a new estimator introduced here; we use a stable log-CDF-difference implementation with convergence diagnostics. The coefficient b is the minimum-force β exponent. The MLE absorbs some inconsistency as residual and uses the 315 + 113 censored rows that the geometric decision below excludes.

### 2.5 The declared compatibility decision

The decision audited in §§4–5 is a separate operational predicate. For each single-speed folder, fix a slope p and allow a free intercept a in log F = a + p log β. A finite bracket in column j becomes the intercept interval I_j = [l_j − p log β_j − ε, u_j − p log β_j + ε], and the folder is *compatible* with p if one a lies in every I_j. Primary settings are K = 2 and ε = 0.2 (a force factor of about 0.819–1.221). p = −2 is the classical minimum-force shape, whereas p = −1.2 is a descriptive reference selected from earlier empirical estimates, not the exact −1 law of [4]. ε is an operational resolution, not a confidence interval or measured noise level.

Membership is fixed from the original labels: only the 1,132 finite columns are eligible, and edited states must also have finite positive brackets, so a column cannot disappear. This prevents trivial all-censored escapes but restricts the estimand to the originally resolved subset. The all-column predicate differs from MLE inference in the rows used, in aggregation over speeds and in its hard tolerance; §6 tests the connection. Protocols were committed locally before the corresponding costs were computed: `37c1a46` (§4), `e94d8e4` and `61b5304` (§§5.1–5.4), `406d44f` (§5.5) and `aed035e` (§6), with `45014c5` for the post hoc certificate-family extension of §6.4.

## 3. Empirical motivation: the measured exponent depends on how regimes are read

### 3.1 Force reference time

Per-session interval-censored fits log F_min = a + b_v log v + b_β log β (t = 0.2 s, K = 2) did not reproduce the development pattern in the four sessions never used for this statistic (window-force β: A3 −1.22, A4 −1.42, D2 −1.11, B_T2 s2 −1.31; development non-A_T1 −1.6 to −2.2), and the same-day repeat pair differed by 0.36, so a single diagram cannot separate β^−1 from β^−2. The window force is affected by a travel-dependent load decrease that is known in this collection [10]. Replacing it by the label-free force at bow onset (11–12 sessions with ramp metadata) gave F_min ∝ v^0.95 [0.86, 1.05] β^−1.22 [−1.36, −1.09] (random effects, HKSJ), between-session SD 0.13/0.18. On the window-force axis the same sessions gave v^1.04–1.09 β^−1.45 to −1.54 with twice the scatter, and the onset-minus-window difference was +0.21 [+0.11, +0.32] in β. Censoring variants moved single sessions between −0.9 and −1.6 but none near −2.

We therefore report a sensitivity, not a law: in the same measured sessions, with the vibration state fixed at the same window, changing only the time at which the force coordinate is read (bow onset vs the analysis window) moves the minimum-force β exponent by ≈0.2 and halves its between-session scatter. The onset-referenced boundary is a command-to-outcome map, not a maintenance-stability limit in the current force, so the two coordinates do not estimate the same quantity and neither is assumed correct. The onset axis was chosen after seeing the data, and speed is confounded with recording block. A v/β law has already been proposed from simulations, together with a dependence of the fitted exponent on the analysis window [4]; establishment and maintenance thresholds differ in bowing-machine measurements [7]; and Schoonderwaldt et al. found measured lower-limit slopes not different from −2 [2]. Comparing the one descending scan (A1) with the other A T1 samples bounds the time-order bias of the β exponent at −0.06 ± 0.06 (onset) and −0.12 ± 0.10 (window), in the direction of a steeper, not shallower, exponent (assumes A1 shares the A T1 exponent; one reversed session).

### 3.2 Label thresholds

A locally planned sweep changed one classifier threshold at a time (window axis, 14 sessions; onset axis, 11 sessions; Fig. 1). Labels rebuilt from the stored window features reproduced every stored session exponent exactly, and an independent re-implementation reproduced the sweep. The periodicity threshold (0.7–0.9) moved the mean exponent by ≤ 0.02. The single-flyback criterion (second flyback below 0.3–0.7 of the first) moved it by −0.17 to +0.17, a span of 0.34 on the window axis and 0.17 on the onset axis. Post hoc, amplitude requirements scaled to the column median Helmholtz amplitude (or to v/β) moved it by ≤ +0.16. No such variant brought the mean near −2 (steepest −1.70, 95 % CI −1.89 to −1.51).

The pre-declared range statistic (S = 2.4 on the window axis and 1.9 on the onset axis, against a "label-dependent" band of S ≥ 0.3) is dominated by a fixed amplitude floor. That floor moved the mean to −0.1 or +0.7 because it removes 42–58 % of Helmholtz windows in the largest-β tercile but 0–6 % in the smallest (measured Helmholtz amplitude ∝ v^0.9 β^−0.75); it changes the β axis itself, and its exclusion is post hoc. Without it the range is 0.34 (window) and 0.17 (onset). Requiring sustained motion shifted the mean exponent by −0.001 to +0.03, because 92–100 % of measured Helmholtz strokes at 0.2 s persist. The conclusion that the measured minimum force falls more gently than β^−2 therefore survives every β-neutral threshold tested, but the value of the exponent is threshold-sensitive at the ±0.17 level, and at large β many columns are left-censored at the lowest force tested.

### 3.3 Criterion response requires force ordering

A fixed-removal experiment on exposed diagrams applied feature thresholds X ≥ Kβ^a, with K chosen within each speed folder to remove approximately 15 % of base-H windows and a varied over a fixed grid. For amplitude over 14 diagrams, the mean exponent shift rose monotonically from −0.65 at a = −1.5 to +0.85 at a = 0, and the neutral point (−1.09) lay within the declared tolerance of 0.25 of the marginal amplitude slope (−0.92). The four broadly available features had the predicted direction in 14/14 diagrams each, but corner width, available only for B1, reversed direction, so an earlier wording "five features, 14/14 each" was incorrect. The ratio curve had no zero crossing in [−1, 1], and the direction did not transfer to the maximum boundary. These are empirical responses, not a universal tilt law.

Marginal feature scaling cannot determine the response. For exactly m removals of the smallest log X − a log β, the sum of log β among removed windows is nondecreasing with a, but this rank property does not imply monotonicity of a boundary exponent. A finite-grid example using the actual K = 2 boundary definition and a nondegenerate interval-normal fit gives an exponent change 0 → −0.11905 when a increases 0 → 1. A second example has a = b = 0 but moves the exponent 0 → +1; permuting its features within one β column preserves the column's feature distribution, the marginal slope, the β and force grids, base labels, removal fraction and removed β columns, yet changes the exponent shift from +1 to 0. The joint ordering of feature and force is therefore indispensable.

A force-conditioned replay on the 13 diagrams (post hoc; four features, 338 correlated diagram–tilt cells, not 338 independent experiments; the retrospective model, grids, 20 Gaussian replications, seed and scoring thresholds were fixed before computing the new outputs) regenerated features from speed-folder intercepts and log β among base-H windows (M0) or additionally from log window force (M1). Neither model fits the observed exponent shifts. Both keep the original H, β and force grid and reapply the 15 % removal, K = 2 extraction and stable interval likelihood. Against the original stored curves, MAE was 0.13267 for M0 and 0.07720 for M1; with matched masks and stable refit targets, 0.13365 → 0.07708 (42.32 %), and the declared gates passed. M1 nevertheless predicted a second-flyback-ratio neutral point at +0.8325 where the observed curve never crossed zero, and the Gaussian surrogate is not a valid joint generator of H waveforms. Replay accuracy is internal to the observed base labels and grids; new-session prediction and causal explanation are untested. Endpoint counts locate the discrete pathway: across amplitude tilts, 43.6 % of originally banded column–tilt pairs changed their lower endpoint, compared with 5.9 % at the upper endpoint (counts reuse columns across tilts). These results motivate auditing the boundary-to-law pipeline directly, through the force-ordered runs themselves.

## 4. Exact label-edit sensitivity

### 4.1 Estimand

For the decision of §2.5, the *reversal distance* is the minimum number of window-label changes (H ↔ non-H) after which the decision differs, subject to the eligibility constraint. The direction depends on the original verdict: for an incompatible folder it is the restoration distance, for a compatible one the exclusion distance. A distance d certifies that fewer than d permitted edits cannot reverse that verdict. This all-column predicate is different from the earlier interval-MLE inference. General fragility indices and restrictions on admissible changes have prior art [14]; our object is the discrete boundary-to-law pipeline, not a new generic fragility concept.

### 4.2 Exact construction

A trailing-run-state dynamic program gives the minimum allowed Hamming cost C_js for every possible first-run start s in column j in O(nK) time. The cost includes creating a later run or removing an earlier one. The program specializes established minimum correction to a regular language [15]. Each candidate bracket becomes an intercept interval I_js = [l_js − p log β_j − ε, u_js − p log β_j + ε], and the exact compatibility-restoration distance is min_a Σ_j min_{s: a ∈ I_js} C_js. A sweep of interval endpoints and open cells finds this minimum without an intercept grid. For the opposite direction, a family of one-dimensional closed intervals has empty intersection exactly when two intervals from different columns are strictly disjoint; minimizing their incremental costs above per-column minima gives the exact exclusion distance. Both bounds are attained by reconstructed local label edits, which serve as explicit witnesses.

### 4.3 Validation

Independent enumeration, which does not use the production dynamic program or sweep, checked 92,238 local endpoint/no-run costs, 3,016 global fixtures (218,318 candidate combinations) and 540 end-to-end cases (107,742 label combinations), all with agreement. Independent reapplication checked 355 feasible primary witnesses and 4,901 listed edits across the 234 directional records. Combinatorial exactness is conditional on ordinary floating-point endpoint comparisons, not exact physical measurement. All 53 unrestricted primary restoration optima have positive-width common-intercept intersections and remain attainable under open-lower brackets.

### 4.4 Results

Under the primary all-column rule, both slopes are initially incompatible in all 39 speed folders, so all primary distances are restoration costs (Fig. 2). For p = −2, compatibility can be restored in 14 folders by 2–20 edits (median 13.5). The median eligible-window edit fraction is 0.884 %, while the median changed-β-column fraction is 38.32 %: a few boundary-deciding labels reach across a wide β range. For p = −1.2, all 39 folders permit restoration, with 5–163 edits (median 26), 1.68 % of eligible windows and 48.28 % of columns at the medians. These are separate sets; comparing their unpaired medians as model selection would be invalid. Even on the common 14 folders, where −2 costs less in 10 cases (medians 13.5 versus 20), 11 folders are at the highest speed and all retain only 13–32 of 40 original columns. Minimum edit cost is not a calibrated physical model-selection score. As an example, B_T2s1 at 0.20 m/s becomes compatible with p = −2 after 12 label changes in 10 β columns, and 11 or fewer permitted changes cannot achieve this; the changes are hypothetical, not corrected observations.

The direction of permitted edits matters. Allowing only H deletions, p = −2 can be restored in 4/39 folders, and allowing only H additions in 8/39; for p = −1.2 the counts are 37/39 and 19/39. In synthetic examples, three random label errors that left the boundary unchanged could still reduce the decision-reversal distance from two to one, and four errors near the boundary excluded the generating law while only one or two edits restored compatibility. The minimum decision-change count is therefore not the number of label errors.

### 4.5 Geometry versus sensitivity

The remaining 25 p = −2 cases are force-grid infeasible under the stated finite-boundary restriction, not infinitely robust physical rejections. Ignoring costs, each column's candidate intercept intervals form a contiguous union. If L_j and U_j are its endpoints before tolerance expansion, feasibility requires max_j L_j − ε ≤ min_j U_j + ε. Thus the post hoc diagnostic ε_grid = max(0, (max_j L_j − min_j U_j)/2) supplies an obstruction certificate from two columns. It agrees with all 78 primary slope–folder geometry decisions; it is not valid as a sufficient condition for disconnected candidate unions. At ε = 0, 0.1, 0.2 and 0.4 the numbers of p = −2 restoration-feasible folders are 12, 13, 14 and 20, and the median bidirectional restoration costs are 41.5, 25, 13.5 and 4. At ε = 0.4, four p = −2 folders and one p = −1.2 folder are already compatible, and their reversal distance is the exclusion cost, not zero. K = 1 and K = 3 were also computed, but because K changes which columns are finite, those are not comparisons on the same population.

### 4.6 Interpretation

Sparse arbitrary window edits may affect many β boundaries. They do not establish actual misclassification, and need not be realizable by a single coherent feature-based classifier; the unrestricted edit distance is a lower bound for that more restricted problem (§5). A hypothetical witness demonstrates sensitivity, not corrected truth. The benefit over an average criterion-response error (§3.3) is an exact, auditable decision guarantee for a precisely stated target, together with a separate support diagnosis. No original confidence interval, frozen score, force measurement or physical claim is replaced.

## 5. Shared waveform rules, joint-rule regions and minimum fixed-column obstructions

### 5.1 Declared scalar rule families

The certificate of §4 permits independent label substitutions. We next change one final waveform-feature gate at a time, applying one absolute scalar threshold to every window in all 39 folders. The primary ranges replace the periodicity floor 0.8 by t ∈ [0.7, 0.9] or the second-flyback-ratio ceiling 0.5 by t ∈ [0.3, 0.7]. Separate uncalibrated stress families add amplitude or flyback-height floors t ∈ [0, +∞] to the original H rule, and additional sensitivities extend periodicity and ratio to [0, 1]. Other gates and waveform extraction remain fixed; folder-specific thresholds, quantile normalization and classifiers using β or force are not allowed. Nonfinite scores are locked to their original labels. All four nominal rules reproduce the 78,000 original labels exactly. The eligible columns, slopes and ε = 0.2 are those of §4. The rule is also applied to originally excluded columns, whose changes count toward the all-data cost, and a folder with any newly unresolved eligible boundary cannot count as compatible.

### 5.2 Complete computation and attainable first boundaries

Sorted threshold sweeps are established [16]; every tied score group must change together. Between consecutive scores, the labels and their downstream boundaries are constant, so enumerating the complete threshold arrangement gives exact costs and attainable decisions for the declared families without observation-specific edits. A structural diagnostic was specified before costs. For H_i(t) = fixed_i OR (guard_i AND score_i ≥ t), let q_s be the minimum effective score in the K adjacent force-ranked windows starting at s. The first run starts at s exactly when max_{r<s} q_r < t ≤ q_s, so only strict record-high q_s values can be first boundaries. A strict upper-score gate gives the dual record-low condition on K-window maxima; fixed labels and infinite endpoints are treated explicitly. This elementary order-statistic reduction is independently implemented; it does not introduce record statistics. Even individually reachable column boundaries can require conflicting common thresholds.

### 5.3 Attainability

At ε = 0.2, the periodicity family restores neither slope in any folder. The ratio family restores p = −2 only in B_T2r1 at 0.10 m/s. The amplitude and height stress families restore p = −1.2 only in B_T2r1 at 0.05 m/s, the same folder for both features. Extending periodicity and ratio to [0, 1] adds no primary-tolerance success. The union across all examined families thus attains 1/14 geometrically feasible p = −2 folders and 1/39 for p = −1.2 (Table 1). The 25 already geometry-infeasible p = −2 folders add no evidence about classifier restrictions. The two successful speed folders belong to the same setup repeat, not independent replication across string types.

Within columns, the restriction is structural. In the primary periodicity range, 1,112/1,132 eligible columns have only one attainable finite first boundary. The median fraction of finite-force candidate starts that can be reached is 2.08 % for periodicity, 6.25 % for ratio, 20.83 % for amplitude and 18.75 % for height. This diagnoses a within-column restriction, not the probability of a correct label or the whole cross-column coherence gap.

### 5.4 Costs and collateral changes

The ratio rule's successful primary range is [0.3, 0.33218555281080747], a positive-width interval. Choosing its least-cost pattern changes 44 of the target folder's 1,200 eligible-window labels across 21/24 eligible β columns (Fig. 3). The arbitrary minimum is 5; because the coherent rule only deletes H labels, the direction-matched arbitrary minimum is 6. Changes total 88 within that complete folder, and 3,200 across all 78,000 windows (4.10 %). Ten eligible boundaries in nine other folders become unresolved. Amplitude and height require respectively 32 and 26 changes in their common 650-window eligible target set, versus arbitrary minima 5 in both directions or 6 deletion-only. Applied globally, they change 12,021 and 11,492 labels, with boundary losses in 23 and 22 other folders. Within-target coherence costs and all-data collateral costs must be distinguished; dividing a global count by a local bound would conflate them. These hypothetical changes do not establish physical errors or damage.

After inspecting collateral losses, a separate post hoc diagnostic required all 1,132 original eligible boundaries across all folders to remain finite-positive. Under that stricter condition no examined family restores any folder at ε = 0.2. This statement must remain tolerance-specific: at ε = 0.4, four p = −2 folders are already compatible, and the ratio rule adds A4 at 0.20 m/s using 150 all-data edits with no boundary loss. Six folders can each be made compatible using different ratio thresholds, but at most five simultaneously, so per-folder optimal thresholds must not be pooled as if they were one common rule.

### 5.5 Joint periodicity/ratio rule

A one-at-a-time gate audit need not cover joint classifier changes. Before calculating the new costs, the local plan fixed one pair (u, v) for every window: unchanged physical gates AND periodicity ≥ u AND second-flyback ratio < v, with u ∈ [0.7, 0.9], v ∈ [0.3, 0.7] and baseline (0.8, 0.5). The fixed eligible population, K = 2, slopes and ε settings are inherited from §4. The physical guard was reconstructed from the cached measurements: 105 observations can activate only when both baseline gates are relaxed, so combining the old scalar guards would omit them. All 78,000 baseline labels and both scalar slices were reproduced. Missing feature values are locked to baseline labels; no original positive needed this fallback.

A K-run activates on an anchored rectangle, u ≤ the minimum periodicity and v > the maximum ratio of its mutable members. Its first-run region subtracts the activation regions of all preceding runs, and a prefix envelope provides an independent rectangle decomposition. Composing these regions with the force brackets gives a complete threshold-cell partition, with finite compatible intercept intervals or explicit obstructions. In each incompatible cell, one invalid eligible column or two separating intercept constraints suffice as a witness. These are familiar rectangle and interval constructions, specialized to the coupled scientific pipeline; related verification and constraint-explanation methods are established [17,18].

The 597,003 local cells combine into 22,282,064 common parameter cells. These are arrangement cells, not independent samples or necessarily distinct label vectors. At ε = 0.2, only B_T2r1 at 0.10 m/s can attain p = −2, with the same 44 eligible / 3,200 all-data edits and nine other unresolved folders as the scalar ratio result. No folder attains p = −1.2 with this pair of gates. Simultaneous gate changes add no feasible folder beyond the scalar slices at any of the four declared ε values. The method does detect joint-only success on independent synthetic fixtures, so the negative empirical result is not a restriction of the search.

### 5.6 Minimum fixed-column obstructions

After seeing the joint outcomes, we searched for the smallest fixed subset of β columns that remains incompatible everywhere in the domain. A pointwise conflicting pair can change with the thresholds, so pointwise interval separation does not prove that one pair suffices uniformly. Exhaustive checks of 33,728 pairs and 5,246 triples show that the 77 joint-incompatible folder–slope cases admit 75 minimum two-column certificates and two minimum three-column certificates. Twenty-five p = −2 failures were already impossible under arbitrary labels because of the force grid and are not new classifier evidence. Among the remaining 52 failures (13 at p = −2 and 39 at p = −1.2), 50 require exactly two fixed columns and two require exactly three. Every singleton is feasible at baseline; all pairs are checked before asserting minimum cardinality three.

The three-column cases are A1 at 0.20 m/s, p = −1.2 (β approximately 0.03207, 0.03828, 0.09288), and B_T2r1 at 0.05 m/s, p = −2 (approximately 0.01969, 0.02094, 0.02996). Their three selected boundaries stay finite everywhere. Different pairs separate on different parameter regions, while the fixed triple certifies the whole domain (Fig. 4); the covers need only 18 and 12 rectangles, respectively. Cardinality three is an empirical result, not a general upper bound. Fifty of the 52 classifier-specific failures have some fixed-pair or fixed-triple certificate relying solely on numerical interval separation throughout the domain; the other two also use invalidity in part of the domain. The minimum counts β columns, not mislabelled observations, independent specimens or causal factors. These post hoc explanations distinguish a changing conflicting pair from one fixed pair, and they name measurement locations that a label review could examine, without implying that those locations were mismeasured or misclassified.

### 5.7 Continuous tolerance exposes a near-cutoff result

In a valid cell the required tolerance is δ = max(0, (L − U)/2), where L and U are the largest lower and smallest upper shifted intercept endpoints before ε expansion. We minimized it across the joint domain, separately with and without preservation of every original eligible boundary (Fig. 5). For p = −2, the smallest all-boundary-preserving value across folders is 0.2055524847973138, at A4 0.20 m/s with (u, v) = (0.8, 0.49326392706532574). This changes one target label and 150 labels globally, with zero unresolved eligible boundaries. The same folder originally requires δ ≈ 0.409165. Direct replay is incompatible at ε = 0.205552 and compatible at 0.205553; the adjacent float64 failure and success values were also checked. Therefore the ε = 0.2 no-loss impossibility is close to a decision cutoff and must not be called strong physical robustness. For p = −1.2, the smallest per-folder minimum δ is 0.227326 without and 0.290378 with boundary preservation; these are not minima for simultaneous compatibility of all folders. δ is an operational tolerance, not a calibrated error estimate or confidence level, and per-folder minimum δ values need not share a common optimizing threshold pair.

### 5.8 Verification

For the scalar families, a direct-classification oracle matched 828 synthetic cases, 34,620 profile comparisons and 43,236 minimum checks, and the record-envelope implementation passed 94,024 endpoint/cell checks. For empirical validation, each column's local feature thresholds were classified afresh and mapped to every shared threshold, independently of the production event updates; all 182,057 shared-threshold patterns and 157,115,191 stored profile scalars agreed. The audit also checked 48 global optima, 288 budget optima and 1,872 folder optima, and reapplied 48 global threshold witnesses to all windows. For the joint rule, independent direct-label calculations checked 755 synthetic fixtures, 90,140 threshold pairs, all empirical local cells and 16,184,036 saved profile values. An exhaustive dominance checker visited all common cells and verified 96 budget optima, 1,248 local optimum/null results and 156 tolerance minima. The fixed-subset certificates were independently checked over all pairs and triples, 1,660 cover rectangles and 75,062 coverage/endpoint probes. These are computational checks; they do not increase the scientific sample size.

## 6. Geometric certificates versus the original censored MLE

### 6.1 Why no implication is expected

The original likelihood (§2.4) pools all 120 β columns across three speeds in each diagram and retains 315 left-censored and 113 right-censored/no-run rows, in addition to the 1,132 finite rows used in the geometric audit. A minimal hard obstruction therefore cannot be interpreted automatically as a minimal MLE-rejection or deletion set. A symmetric synthetic construction in the full three-speed design requires geometric tolerance 0.95 yet has an optimal free exponent exactly equal to the fixed target and likelihood contrast zero. At specified σ, a separated same-speed pair with shifted-interval gap g > 0 does impose an absolute negative log-likelihood of at least −2 log Φ(−g/(2σ)); it does not bound the contrast with the free model.

### 6.2 Design

A local pre-scoring plan fixed deletion of the previously selected certificates, all same-cardinality deletions among eligible columns of the same speed folder as controls, and a secondary deletion of corresponding β-index blocks across speeds. We reproduced all 13 original full-data stable fits (maximum parameter difference 1.11 × 10⁻⁷), then enumerated 22,110 distinct deletion sets and 60,234 numerical fits. For each remaining dataset we also profiled the intercept, speed exponent and scale under b = p and recorded T = 2(NLL_fixed − NLL_free). T and deletion ranks are descriptive; no selected-subset p-values or replacement bootstrap confidence intervals are claimed.

### 6.3 Selected certificates

Among the 52 classifier-specific cases, deleting the previously selected minimum certificate changed b by a median absolute 0.00613 (Fig. 6a). The estimate moved toward the target in 17 cases and away in 35. Its median absolute-influence rank was 54.2 % among all size-matched deletions (34.7 % among controls with log-β span within 20 %); two cases reached the upper 5 %. T decreased in 37 and increased in 15. These dependent cases do not constitute independent replications, and removing a certificate need not remove all geometric obstructions. An exact likelihood-accounting identity separates the removed rows' full-fit contrast from the differential gain from refitting nuisance parameters. For B_T2r2 at 0.05 m/s, p = −1.2, b changed from −1.816 to −1.955 and T from 54.55 to 98.79: the removed-row and refitting terms in the T reduction were −25.245 and −18.987. Removing the columns that carried the geometric conflict thus moved the fit farther from the candidate law; this does not mean those columns are erroneous.

### 6.4 The entire minimum-certificate family

A separately declared post hoc extension evaluated every previously enumerated minimum certificate: 7,718 pairs and 24 triples. Of the 52 classifier-specific families (3,599 certificates), 12 move b away from the target for every member, six increase T for every member, and 31 contain both signs of T change. In both minimum-three cases, all possible triples move away from the target and increase T: 19/19 for A1 at 0.20 m/s, p = −1.2, and 5/5 for B_T2r1 at 0.05 m/s, p = −2. Thus the non-implication is not merely an unlucky lexicographic certificate choice. Selecting the largest T reduction within the minimum-cardinality geometric family gives an explanation relevant to the fitted model; the best member reduces T in 46 of the 52 cases and increases it even at best in six. This selection is outcome-selected, does not solve minimum MLE reversal, and carries no inferential calibration.

### 6.5 Coherent-rule replay

Two previously identified shared waveform rules were also replayed without optimizing their MLE effects. The no-loss A4 witness, with 150 global label changes, reduces the target folder's geometric tolerance from 0.409165 to 0.205552 but moves the full-diagram MLE from b = −1.41687 to −1.40459 and increases T for p = −2 from 32.216 to 34.944 (Fig. 6b). Two A4 boundary intervals change, including one left-censored row becoming finite; the full MLE uses information excluded from the hard predicate. Over all 13 diagrams this rule changes b by at most 0.01547. The other designated rule, (0.8, 0.3321855528), changes 3,200 labels and loses 10 originally finite eligible boundaries in nine folders; it changes b by at most 0.28690, and the MLE retains the affected columns as one-sided censored rows rather than dropping them. Geometric improvement therefore need not imply likelihood improvement even for one consistent classifier rule.

### 6.6 Verification and scope

An independent implementation checked all interval identities and control objectives, 779 independent fits, all 7,742 certificate joins and 23,226 family-member ranks. It used the established inverse-scale convex parameterization [19,20], distinct from the production optimizer; maximum parameter differences were about 1.31 × 10⁻⁶ and NLL differences at most 4.61 × 10⁻¹¹, with zero unresolved fit failures. Complete deletion enumeration is not an exact real-arithmetic MLE certificate. All results are retrospective and conditional on the specified rule and observation models; the original confidence-interval decisions are not reversed.

## 7. Discussion

### 7.1 What is new and what is not

Covariate-adjusted thresholds [12], artificial scaling caused by detection thresholds [13] and interval-censored likelihood [11] are established. So are minimum correction to a regular language [15], fragility indices with restricted modifications [14], sorted threshold sweeps [16], multiverse analysis of processing choices [21] and its geometric characterization [22], rectangle-based robustness verification [17], minimal contradiction explanations [18], the approximate maximum influence perturbation (AMIP) for dropping a little data [23], testing of most influential sets [24], and convex parameterizations of censored-likelihood regression [19,20]. Predictive multiplicity typically imposes predictive-error admissibility [25], which we cannot establish without independent true-motion labels. Regime-classification and analysis-window effects already have quantitative bowed-string prior art [4]. We therefore claim neither the general threshold-bias phenomenon, threshold sweeping, two-dimensional threshold search, minimal explanations, deletion influence nor a new censoring estimator.

The candidate contribution is the exact, verified composition of these tools along one scientific pipeline: shared waveform rule → force-ordered first K-run → cross-column law decision → collateral boundary validity → original censored likelihood. Its specific elements are (i) exact decision-reversal distances for run-defined force boundaries, with witnesses and a separate force-grid support diagnosis; (ii) a record-envelope characterization of the first boundaries attainable by a shared threshold, which partly explains, within columns, why small arbitrary-edit bounds are rarely realized by the examined rules; (iii) minimum fixed-location explanations over a whole classifier domain, combined with a continuous tolerance diagnostic; and (iv) a tested separation between such geometric proofs and influence on the physical-law MLE. Novelty is bounded by the literature reviewed; we have not established worldwide priority.

The results qualify interpretation in both directions. "A few label changes would restore β^−2" overstates classifier-level sensitivity: for p = −2 at ε = 0.2, arbitrary edits succeed in 14 folders and the examined shared rules in one. "No shared rule restores β^−2 without collateral losses" is an exact statement about the declared families at ε = 0.2, but it lies near a transition at 0.205553 and is not strong physical robustness. Neither statement transfers automatically to the fitted law, because the MLE weighs censored rows and residuals that the hard predicate ignores. Physically, the analyses do not decide between β^−1-like and β^−2 minimum-force laws. They show that in this collection the minimum-force β exponent is sensitive by ≈0.2 to the force reference time and at the ±0.17 level to β-neutral thresholds, that the session-mean exponent of every β-neutral variant tested stays more gentle than β^−2, and that this statement rests partly on left-censored columns at large β.

### 7.2 Recommendations for users of robotic Schelleng data

- Report boundaries on both onset-force and window-force axes until the cause of the travel-dependent decrease is resolved.
- Report censored and no-run columns separately; no-run is not a censoring direction.
- Report criterion-response curves and which first/last K-runs they alter; matching marginal feature scaling alone is insufficient.
- Distinguish arbitrary label-change bounds from changes achievable by an explicit shared classifier. Count edits and lost boundaries outside the target condition as well.
- Separate force-grid infeasibility from label sensitivity, and report how a compatibility verdict changes with tolerance rather than at a single cutoff.
- Report the likelihood influence of a geometric obstruction separately and descriptively; a minimal obstruction is not automatically evidence against the fitted law.

### 7.3 Limitations

- **Scope.** One robot, one monochord, cello G strings. Nothing here is a violin measurement; violin statements in the literature [2,3] are cited, not tested.
- **Small samples and dependence.** Thirteen diagrams; speed, session, date, tension and scan direction are confounded, and diagrams are not necessarily independent specimens. Both shared-rule successes come from one setup repeat. Computational counts do not increase the scientific sample size.
- **Exposure.** All analyses are post hoc on exposed data; plans, where they exist, are local only, and several diagnostics were added after seeing results; no unused confirmation set remains.
- **Labels and forces.** Waveform-proxy labels without independent true labels; zero-offset uncertainty of tens of mN below 0.2 N. K = 2, the first-run lower boundary and no-run-as-right-censored are operational conventions.
- **Criterion replay.** Same-session features and known base labels are used. Gaussian tails can violate base-H acceptance, and second-flyback-ratio neutrality is not reproduced. This does not establish physical label accuracy or new-string generalization.
- **Exact-edit scope.** Section 4 uses a retrospective all-column tolerance rule and originally finite positive boundaries. Its arbitrary independent label edits are not calibrated errors or necessarily achievable by a shared classifier. Support infeasibility and edit costs must not be ranked as physical evidence. It does not reverse the earlier MLE test.
- **Coherent-rule scope.** Sections 5.1–5.4 search one scalar final gate at a time; §5.5 additionally certifies the declared joint periodicity/ratio rectangle. Independent accuracy, other feature combinations, alternative waveform extraction and β-dependent classifiers are not certified. The no-boundary-loss condition was added post hoc in the scalar audit and included in the local pre-cost plan for the joint audit.
- **Minimum obstruction scope.** Fixed-subset explanations are post hoc and conditional on the declared classifier domain, fixed eligible population and all-column tolerance predicate. They identify conflicting measurement locations, not erroneous observations or physical causes. Minimum cardinality three in two cases is not a universal bound.
- **MLE bridge scope.** Section 6 performs descriptive, outcome-exposed influence diagnostics. Selecting the most influential minimum obstruction does not establish statistical significance or physical causation; the original confidence-interval decision has not been reversed.
